SCP lane: coverage of SCP Wiki anomaly types in MARK1 (2026-11-22)

Entries: 311 rows in scp.tsv (301 SCP items + 10 object-class labels).
Counts by verdict: COVERED 155, PACK 83, LAW 51, SECOND 8, EXCLUDED 14.
Item number, title and link only; mechanisms are my own one-liners. MARK1 should invent its own entities.

HOW IT WAS CRAWLED
- Fetched the full tag index (system:page-tags: 1011 distinct tags), the Object Classes guide, series hubs I to IX (about 8900 titles), and tag pages for about 150 anomaly-type, class and creature tags (cached in ~/.cache/mark1-build/coverage/scp/raw/; 1 request per second, UA naming a research crawl).
- BFS: index -> tag -> tag page numbers (up to 100 per page) -> joined to series-hub titles. Tag membership of every cached item gave multi-tag cross-references (about 8500 main-series items).
- SAMPLING (no silent caps): tags with up to 6 pages were read in full; larger tags (humanoid 29 pages was full; others) were read as pages 1, middle and last. From the pool, 3 to 10 items per anomaly tag were taken evenly spaced across the number range (so all series I to IX are hit), then deduplicated; 60 percent of the sample was kept to fit the 300 cap, and about 46 well-known items were added by hand (173, 096, 682, 055, 087, 106, 049, 3008, 2000, 231, 914, 500, 1000, 008, 354, 3000 and so on).
- NOT crawled: Tales, GoI formats, JP/KO/etc. branches (they appear only as tags), foundation personnel hubs. Joke, Archived and Explained pages only lightly sampled.
- Reliability: every mechanism not hand-checked against the article text is marked (u). Only the added famous items are mostly known from memory; the sampled items were classified from title plus tag set, so treat the verdicts as family-level, not article-level.

TAG SIZE SIGNAL (cached main-series counts, rough): humanoid 2391, cognitohazard 812, ectoentropic 696, reality-bending 540, memory-affecting 484, temporal 470, teleportation 452, spatial 423, memetic 411, antimemetic 255. So most of SCP is "a person or object with one odd rule", which is PACK or COVERED.

TOP MISSING LAWS (count of LAW/SECOND rows)
1. ME (17): memory-record edit/erase effect. Antimemetics are the biggest single family. A candidate: K memories as editable records, with an M neural-bias effect that blocks encoding or recall. This is likely one law, not 17.
2. IH (13): percept-as-payload. A sensory pattern that triggers an effect in any mind that decodes it (096, memetic images, killer text). Needs a rule that delivery costs the sender nothing per victim, bounded by a ledger, otherwise it breaks the energy budget.
3. CONC (7): concept or meme as an entity living only in minds. Partly covered by beings with believer-substrates; the gap is a being with no physical pattern at all.
4. PAST (7, SECOND): affecting or rewinding the past, whole-world rewind (2000), time travel. Matches the stated second change.
5. PROB (5): luck, bias of chaotic outcomes. A small M effect on chaos with a ledger may do it; otherwise SECOND.
6. FUT (4, SECOND): precognition and prophecy.
7. TIMEDIL (4): local time rate or loops beyond X rewind.
8. RETCON (2, SECOND): global history rewrite.
9. SCALE: giants, planet-size organisms and shrinking need a mass bank and square-cube rule; recorded under PACK rows with the SCALE note.

SURPRISING
- The big lump is not "magic" but information: ME and IH together are 30 of 59 LAW/SECOND rows. MARK1's K and M layers mostly cover SCP; what is missing is a typed way for a pattern to be harmful by being perceived or remembered.
- Only 8 rows need a true second change. Most SCP time-and-space oddities map to X (doorways, planes, rewind of parts together).
- Object classes are social labels (S layer), not anomalies; they match MARK1's fear from unpredictability (Euclid) and danger estimates in K.
- Antimemetic horror is almost free in MARK1's narrator rule (it shows only what a character perceives) if memory records can be edited; the fear source is that the player and character both forget.
