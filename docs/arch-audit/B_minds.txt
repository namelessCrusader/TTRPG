0. THE PICTURE IN TEN LINES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. src/mind (about 3,000 lines of code, 587-line catalog test) is a clean,
   stdlib-only, TASK-LEVEL interface, and two backends use it: src/rooms
   (a fork of the lab inn) and src/voxel/mindlink.py (voxel bodies).
2. The only things that choose through it are STATELESS choosers: Rules,
   Random, Gate, Combined (a text model plus trait bias), Replay, Player,
   Tourney (loop.py:84 NPC table; choosers.py).
3. The learned mind (lab/mindL, 8,497 lines with tests and reports) is a
   STATEFUL agent written against the lab inn's own world object
   (w.log, w.here, w.perceived_need, w.drive_error ...). It cannot be a
   Chooser as the protocol is written (item 1).
4. About 890 lines of it (actor_critic, world_model, habit, valence,
   curiosity, episodic, effects, concerns) import NOTHING from the world:
   numpy and the standard library only. The world is reached through two
   adapters, reader.py and drives.py, plus Mind._readings and
   Agent.perceived_at_me (item 2). This is the best news in the lane.
5. The voxel backend has NO needs: catalog drives `hunger` and `thirst` are
   `voxel: null`, `safety` is a binary percept test, `harm` is a scripted
   per-name weight (verbs.yaml:97-102; mindlink.py:287-301). It offers 14
   verbs (door, walk, take, strike, shout, wait), the inn has 24 plus 9.
   So "the learned mind into src" is two separate jobs: the plumbing
   (doable now, on the rooms backend) and the voxel life (blocked on the
   body-needs reader, 15c bodies and 16a primitives) (items 3, 4).
6. The rooms fork is stale: forked at lab/world2/world.py sha1 0cea595,
   now 66084f1; 806 lines here against 2,148 in the lab; it has none of
   needs, meals, partner, news, calm, signs, hard, lethal, perma, field,
   serve, tend (grep: 0 hits). The lab inn moved on for two months.
7. Evidence: the learned mind does not yet beat the rules in the lab
   (step 2 L1/L2: effects -0.058, labels +0.240, rules +0.380; survival
   check not met, 0 deaths in 600 person-years, secondary measures FAVOUR
   the oracle; concern weights at noise level, about 0.04). The interface
   work is worth doing now; porting the CLAIM is not (item 9).
8. Rules are spread over four places that do not share code: the backend's
   `rule_scores` (mindlink.py:671, rooms/backend.py:246), the Rules chooser
   (T=0.2), the voxel reflex TablePolicy (policy.py:363), and the dormant
   src/core NPC mind (item 8).
9. The LM story in src is thin: NO live model chooser exists in src (only
   record and replay; no src/mind/remote.py), and the only live LM code is
   the old engine's ChoiceLM in src/core/llm.py. Player parse, addressed-NPC
   wording and narration are not built (item 12).
10. The most expensive work in the lane is not code but a choice: where the
   learned mind runs FIRST in the real engine and where its code lives
   (items 3 and 5 are owner-level).

PART I. THE MIND INTERFACE: WHAT MUST CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ITEM 1. THE CHOOSER PROTOCOL CANNOT HOLD A LEARNING MIND
What exists
  - src/mind/api.py:312 Chooser: submit(DecisionRequest), poll(id).
    api.py:271 DecisionRequest: option TEXTS, group names, and the three
    rule-score tuples. Header rule (api.py:19-21): "a chooser gets a
    DecisionRequest and nothing more ... holds no Ref, no tick".
  - loop.py:189-243 Mind.decide builds the Menu, throws the Option objects
    away after the request is made, asks, and tells the backend.
  - tourney.py/policy.batchable: a chooser is asked about SUB-MENUS and the
    winners merged; this assumes asking changes nothing (will.py:903
    `batchable`, policy.py:383).
  - choosers.py:59-70: draws are seeded from (seed, who, request id), no
    state.
Problem
  A learned mind needs four things the protocol deliberately hides:
  (a) WHO the options are about (a Ref per role): valence, partner models,
      concerns and the actor's U[verb, target] table are all keyed by
      person. DecisionRequest strips identities on purpose (to keep model
      prompts clean). The two purposes collide in one type.
  (b) OUTCOMES: what happened between this choice and the next (the events
      it perceived, the change in its own needs). There is no channel back
      to a chooser; Why/Job events go to the log, not to the mind.
  (c) STATE ACROSS DECISIONS and a place to keep it for snapshots: lab
      Mind holds ActorCritic, Habit, Valence, WorldModel, PartnerModels,
      Episodic, Curiosity, DeltaMeanings, RWConcerns and an open Step
      (mind.py:113-218). Nothing in src snapshots or restores a chooser;
      the voxel link has `on_restore` that only FORGETS pending requests
      (mindlink.py:1155).
  (d) NOT being asked twice or in pieces. A learner that is asked about
      batches of a menu would "act" several times per decision (and the
      tournament would update the actor on sub-picks that never happen).
  It also has a randomness problem: lab Mind uses a stateful
  np.random.default_rng (mind.py:119); the sim rule is NO generator, draws
  from hash of (seed, who, id) (design 2.4; choosers.seed_of). A stateful
  rng is not restored by a snapshot, so a replay after restore diverges.
Better options
  A. (recommended) Add a second, richer contract beside Chooser, in
     src/mind/api.py, and make Chooser a special case of it:
        Mind (one per character, stateful):
          observe(obs)      percepts it perceived, job events, its own body
                            state, readings of others' signs; no world access
          decide(ask)  -> Decision    ask = the typed Menu (Options with
                            Refs and features), SelfState, Needs, a clock;
                            pure of side effects on the WORLD, may update
                            its own working state
          close(outcome)    the step closes (next choice, death, sleep):
                            reward inputs, learning
          sleep(span)       consolidation (replay), tied to a real sleep
                            JOB, not a loop boundary
          state()/restore(s)  plain data (arrays and dicts), snapshot-safe
          explain(decision) -> typed terms for the cause log
     Chooser adapters: ChooserMind(chooser) ignores observe/close/sleep and
     keeps today's behaviour byte for byte (so goldens do not move).
     Draws come from seed_of(seed, who, decision counter), the counter being
     part of state().
  B. Keep Chooser, add optional hooks read with getattr (the voxel policy
     style: observe, prepare, limbs). Cheaper, already the house style, but
     unchecked: a typo in a hook name silently becomes "no learning". Only
     acceptable if a conformance suite (item 6) checks that every hook the
     mind declares is called.
  C. Make the learned mind a Backend-side policy (as lab does it). Rejected:
     it forks the logic per backend, the opposite of one interface (PA4).
Cost and risk
  Small: one protocol file, one adapter, loop.py change in `decide` (pass
  the Menu through instead of dropping it), tests. Risk: the "no Ref in a
  request" rule exists for the model prompt; keep DecisionRequest for text
  models and add a separate typed ask for Minds, so nothing about the model
  path changes. Risk 2: a stateful mind in a snapshot makes snapshot size
  and restore time a mind concern (weights are small: see item 2).
Status: NEW (K1 asks for a replaceable mind interface but not this shape).
Priority: P1. Technical.

ITEM 2. THE LEARNED MIND IS NEARLY WORLD-FREE; THE FIVE HARD-WIRED TABLES
ARE THE ONLY BLOCKER
What exists (lab/mindL)
  World-free, numpy only: actor_critic.py (89), world_model.py (34),
  habit.py (32), valence.py (35), curiosity.py (47), episodic.py (52),
  effects.py (306), concerns.py (293): about 890 lines.
  World-bound, and why:
    reader.py:28-43   VERBS = sorted(DUR) (inn verb names), PEOPLE = the CAST
                      of five plus "the bandit", TARGETS one-hot of fixed
                      size NT, KEYS = 43 inn option-feature names (ale, anger,
                      armed ... goal_*), ZDIM = 1 + 43 + 7.
    drives.py:25-76   DRIVES from world2 (7), ROOMS one-hot in the critic
                      state, NATURE[kind] = a (weight, set point) table keyed
                      by the inn's TYPE STRING (peasant, knight, merchant,
                      thief, cook, bandit), TURNS = 300.
    partners.py:46    KIND_ACTS and HARSH_ACTS verb lists (the labels that
                      SV3 replaces by effects, still the control arm).
    mind.py:310-326   Mind._readings calls w.here, w.perceived_need,
                      w.held, w.loose, w.things; Agent.perceived_at_me scans
                      w.log; H.state reads w.danger, w.wealth, w.here, w.t.
    mind.py:64-93     DEFAULT has about 20 settings, 12 of them "off so every
                      older run reproduces" (traces, effects, fx_fix,
                      concerns, b_trust, pm_forget, pm_detect, cn_things ...).
    Time unit is the inn TURN everywhere: effects.CREDIT = 12 turns,
    WINDOW = 30 turns, habit fade per NIGHT, replay at the night's end.
Problem
  (a) A new verb (a 16a macro, a pack verb, a cached plan, a mod verb) has no
      row: W[verb], M[verb] are fixed-size arrays sized at import. Content
      as data is blocked by code constants.
  (b) A new person has no slot in U[verb, target] or the one-hot; the
      partner dicts grow but the actor table does not. An open world has
      unbounded, changing people.
  (c) The feature columns are the inn's, by name. The voxel backend and the
      rooms backend give Options NO features at all (rooms/backend.py:41:
      "an Option of this backend has no features"). So the reader has nothing
      to read outside the lab inn.
  (d) NATURE keyed by type string is a trait flag by another name. The
      owner's principle (learned-mind-principles): no trait flags, traits are
      dials plus history plus state. Weights and set points must be
      per-character dials (AU3 `needs and drives` component), not a lookup by
      "knight".
  (e) Turn-based time constants cannot be moved to a world where acts take
      ticks of 50 ms and a day is a real cycle. gamma^dt, CREDIT, WINDOW,
      fade per night all need a world-time unit (seconds of world time).
  (f) The 12 off-by-default arms are the lab's reproducibility device (flag
      off = bit for bit). In src they are dead branches and a test matrix.
Better options
  1. Extract the world-free learners into one package, ported with their
     tests, with open-ended keys: verb rows allocated on first sight from the
     catalog (or, better, a verb is READ as a vector: catalog group, `serves`
     drives, flags, plus declared features, so a pack verb works with no
     new row and rows are shared by similar verbs). People and things are
     keyed by Ref id in dicts or hashed slots, never one-hot. Target
     identity for the actor = the same Ref id.
  2. Replace KEYS by CATALOG-DECLARED FEATURE CHANNELS: a verb states which
     numeric features its options carry (name, range, meaning, source), the
     backend fills them; reader builds z from the declaration. This is the
     same data-not-code move as the catalog itself, and it makes the reader
     the same function in every world.
  3. Drives and set points per character from the entity file; the drive
     LIST comes from the catalog `drives:` block (today 4 words) extended to
     the 7 numeric needs, each backend declaring how it reads them (item 4).
  4. World-time units: seconds. The inn declares its turn length in seconds;
     the lab keeps working through a conversion constant.
  5. One MindSpec (data) naming the modules and their settings; each module
     is a plugin with terms(ctx), learn(step), night/sleep, state(); the
     choice is the sum of plugin terms (Mind.terms at mind.py:220-308 already
     is that sum, written as a chain of `if "X" in m`). Ablations stay a lab
     tool (a spec without the plugin). Dead arms (the control labels arm,
     b_trust, the non-trace critic) are deleted from the src copy once the
     winning configuration is frozen.
Cost and risk
  Medium: about 900 lines move; the reader/drives rewrite is the real work.
  Risk: silently changing the learner while generalising it. Mitigation: the
  lab goldens (golden_traces_off.json) and the lab tests (test_mind.py 613,
  test_effects 435, test_concerns 445, test_tournament 379) become a
  REFERENCE: the ported mind must reproduce a lab night bit for bit under the
  adapter that restores the inn's tables. That is the same sha-pinned
  golden discipline the rooms fork already uses.
Status: NEW. Priority: P1. Technical, except where the code lives
(item 5, owner-level).

ITEM 3. THE VOXEL BACKEND HAS NO NEEDS, SO THERE IS NOTHING TO LEARN FROM
What exists
  verbs.yaml:97-102: drives hunger and thirst `voxel: null`; safety =
  "percept_any" test; harm = "sheet: harm". mindlink.py:287-301 self_state;
  mindlink.py:264-286 fears(), _harm(). mindlink.py:671-719 rule_scores:
  a hand table (strike = harm weight x 1.5 armed else 0.5; take 0.8; flee
  safety x (1 - danger); walk 0.1; wait 0.2; door replies 0.9 / 0.6 / 0.4),
  prior_avg = prior (traits not read, Q5), habit 0. Voxel catalog verbs: 14.
  Physiology and bodies exist (physiology.py, bodyheat, vasculature,
  bleeding, firstaid) but nothing exposes them as drives.
Problem
  The learned mind's reward is the DROP in weighted drive error (H module).
  In the voxel there is no drive error: no energy balance, hydration,
  warmth, pain, fatigue, company or curiosity vector is read. Without it the
  learned mind has nothing to maximise and the rules have nothing to serve:
  today a calm voxel man never does anything except walk out of a door
  "rarely, by choice" (mindlink.py:140-148). Also: the voxel `harm` drive is
  a PER-NAME weight on a character sheet (scripted grudges), the opposite of
  "concerns are acquired, never declared".
  Nothing here is a mind problem alone: it needs the body.
Better options
  A. A BODY-NEEDS READER in the backend: a typed vector, each need read from
     the body's own ledgers (energy reserve and glycogen/fat, water, core
     temperature against comfort, pain and injury from damage records,
     fatigue and sleep debt from the body clock, breath from the O2 chain),
     with each need's SET POINT a body/dial value, and the reading carrying
     the same signs (SV2) others can perceive. The reward is then the drop in
     this error, from real physiology, no designer reward (matches
     learned-mind-principles: blind rewards).
  B. Needs that are not bodily (company, standing, curiosity, wealth) come
     from the mind's own acquired concerns and the world's social ledgers
     (SV1), not from a body register. The lab's 7 inn drives become: 4 bodily
     (energy, safety/pain, rest, warmth) + 3 social/cognitive that are
     LEARNED or from culture packs. Do not invent a voxel "company need".
  C. Until 15c (bodies) and 16a (primitives) land, run the learned mind on
     the rooms backend only (item 4).
Cost and risk
  Reader: small once the body ledgers are final (15c: the heart/perfusion
  model, adult bodies, medical function records feed it). Risk: building the
  reader against the stick-figure body now, then rebasing (EA2 rebases the
  body once). Do the reader AFTER 15c's body slice, before 16a.
Status: partly implied (SC6 needs, EA8 senses) but the NEEDS VECTOR as the
learned mind's input is NEW. Priority: P1 for design, build after 15c.
Technical, with the scope question in item 4.

ITEM 5. WHERE THE SHARED LEARNER CODE LIVES (the isolation rule)
What exists
  lab/README.md and sim-mind-design.md 2.1: "lab and src never import each
  other; they exchange JSONL files". Reason given: the lab venv has torch and
  model packages that must not touch the sim environment, plus the laptop's
  RAM and heat. The learned mind is numpy only.
Problem
  The rule was made for a torch boundary. The learners do not cross it, but
  the rule still forces two copies: either the learner is written once in lab
  and COPIED into src (a sha-pinned fork like rooms: drift every time a lab
  result changes a module, and this is exactly where the lab is most active),
  or it is written in src and the lab imports it (forbidden).
Options
  1. A neutral numpy-only package that BOTH import (for example `mindcore/`
     at the repo root; neither src nor lab). Keeps the torch isolation (it
     never imports torch), removes the fork, one test suite. Needs a one-line
     amendment of the isolation rule: "only the numpy/stdlib-only package
     mindcore is shared".
  2. Keep the rule; port by copy at milestones with a sha pin and a
     byte-for-byte golden (as rooms). Zero rule change; fork drift cost on
     every lab change (and the lab learner is still changing: concerns
     constants unsourced, effects b_partner untuned).
  3. Move the lab's learner into src/mind (it is the destination) and have
     the lab call it through JSONL cards. Impractical: the lab runs 100k
     decisions a night in-process.
Recommendation: option 1. If the owner prefers the strict rule, option 2
with the golden, but then freeze the learner modules in the lab at the moment
of the port and say so in the lab README.
Cost: one amendment and a directory move; risk: the venv boundary (mindcore
must never import torch; add a test that imports it in a clean numpy-only
interpreter).
Status: NEW. Priority: P1 (decide before item 2 starts). OWNER-LEVEL
(principle: a rule you set, with a real reason).

ITEM 6. A MIND CONFORMANCE SUITE (one acceptance test for rules, scripted,
learned and model minds)
What exists
  Tests are per-piece: tests/test_mind_core.py (462), test_mind_loop (540),
  test_mind_scope (547), test_mindlink (836), test_mind_record (298),
  test_mind_catalog (587); lab/mindL/test_mind.py (613) includes
  test_mind_inputs_hold_no_hidden_fact (perturb unperceived facts, state,
  menu and reward must not change). AU2 already plans a conformance suite
  for LAW mods; there is none for minds.
Problem
  Each new mind kind (a scripted scene mind, a learned mind, a pack mind, a
  model mind) would be tested by hand, and the three most valuable tests
  live only in the lab.
Better option
  One suite, run for every mind kind registered, checking:
    1. DETERMINISM: same seed and same inputs, same decisions; a snapshot
       taken mid-run and restored replays identically (draws from a counter
       in state, not a generator).
    2. NO HIDDEN FACT: the lab test, ported: change anything the character
       did not perceive; observe(), decide() inputs and the reward are
       unchanged (the god-channel ablation made this the master rule).
    3. LEGAL ONLY: the pick is always an index into the offered menu; a
       blocked option is never picked.
    4. THE LATENCY LEDGER: model calls per tick by purpose (item 12) are
       zero in reactive mode.
    5. COST CEILING: ms per decision, and ms per decision at 100 characters
       (item 7), p99 as well as mean (EA7).
    6. STATE ROUND TRIP: state() then restore() is the identity.
    7. EXPLAIN: explain() returns typed terms that sum to the score.
Cost: small once item 1 exists (it is the item 1 test). Status: NEW, matches
AU2's pattern. Priority: P1 (written WITH item 1). Technical.

ITEM 7. PER-TICK AND PER-DECISION COST; THE BUDGET AND THE PLACES IT LEAKS
What exists and what is NOT measured
  Measured elsewhere: the crowd kernel timing is 7.41 ms (batch 1 end
  checks); the old per-decision measure "157 against 151 us" is for the
  batched belief view only (sim-mind-design.md 3.4). I could not find any
  recorded cost of Mind.decide in src or of the learned mind per decision in
  either world, and I did not run anything. UNMEASURED: the learned mind's
  ms per decision (27 options x ZDIM 51 einsums, kNN episodic lookup,
  partner logistic per social option, an optional effects and concerns term)
  and the link's own bind() per voxel decision (routes, doors, things).
  Decision frequency is low: the duel prototype asked the mind 8 questions
  in 150 ticks (sim-mind-design 3.5); a task-level mind asks when a job
  ends, not at every reaction time.
Where it leaks, from reading the code
  a. loop.py:208-215 builds the full scene text (describe.describe) and a
     DecisionRequest for EVERY decision, including reactive NPCs whose chooser
     is Rules and reads no text. loop.py:235 appends a row with situation
     text, option lists and probabilities for every job, kept in a list
     (`self.rows`) with no bound: at 100 NPCs over hours this is memory and
     string work for nothing. (Likely, not measured.)
  b. core.menu is built with the pre-rank cut at 16 (core.py:54) by
     max(prior, prior_avg) + habit: the RULES decide what the chooser may
     see. For a learned mind that is a rule veto (item 9).
  c. Every Option is a frozen, validated, sorted-pairs object (api.py:130+),
     built per decision per option: a deliberate trade for hashability.
  d. Level of detail (SC4) is not yet in the mind: a far NPC would call the
     same decide path.
Better option
  1. Build the budget as an acceptance number, not a hope: add decisions per
     simulated second, mean and p99 ms per decision, rows memory per hour to
     the timing harness (the one that reports crowd 7.41 ms). Target to set
     by the owner after measuring; suggestion: the whole mind layer at most
     5 percent of a tick at 20 awake NPCs and 1 ms at p99 per decision.
  2. Lazy text: build the scene text and request only when the chooser or a
     log level needs it (a Rules or learned mind needs neither). Rows to a
     bounded ring or a JSONL sink.
  3. LEVEL-OF-DETAIL TIERS for minds (the SC4 detail ladder, applied to
     cognition): NEAR (full decide, learning on), ROUTINE (the mind is asked
     at routine boundaries only, a habit-level pick, step rewards summed over
     the span), OFFSCREEN (a twin: the SC5 coarse protocol advances the
     character's needs and schedule; the mind state is not touched except by
     one summary close() at hand-back). The learned mind's `sleep(span)` and
     `close(outcome)` take a SPAN so a coarse stretch is one update, with a
     measured drift budget (EA7).
Cost: 1 and 2 small; 3 is the design piece, and it waits for SC4/SC5 (16+).
Status: 1 and 2 NEW; 3 is SC4/SC5 applied to minds (scheduled, details NEW).
Priority: P2 for 1-2, P2 for 3. Technical.

ITEM 8. RULES LIVE IN FOUR PLACES AND ONE OF THEM IS A TRAIT TABLE
What exists
  - Backend rule_scores: mindlink.py:671-719 (hand table, SCORE at :135,
    NO_REASON -1.0 at :149, door replies :168) and rooms/backend.py:246
    (the inn's utility, forked from lab/world2/policy.py:34-133).
  - choosers.Rules: softmax((prior + habit)/T), T = 0.2.
  - voxel reflex TablePolicy (policy.py:363-436): a table keyed by percept,
    a seam of a different shape (per limb, `pick(situation, menu)`).
  - src/core mind.py: traits, salience bands, drive-utility selector
    (dormant, "no new code imports it").
  - Catalog `serves` (verbs.yaml fields) names which drive a verb relieves;
    used only to write scene text and "what is missing", never to score.
  - Lab NATURE per type string; voxel `sheet.harm` by NAME.
Problem
  A new backend must write its own scoring from nothing; there is no single
  statement of "what a character with these needs, in this state, prefers".
  The same idea (a verb that serves a drive relieves it) is written in the
  catalog, in the voxel table, in the inn's `utility`, and in the lab's
  H module, four ways. The type-keyed NATURE and the name-keyed `sheet.harm`
  are trait flags (against the owner's rule) and make traits a thing you
  write per person.
Better option
  ONE RULES MIND, written as data over the catalog: score(option) = sum over
  drives d of weight_d(character) x need_d x serves(verb, d) x relief
  estimate, plus the declared feature terms; weights and set points are the
  character's DIALS (AU3). It is the same homeostatic form the learned mind
  starts from (drives.reward), so the learned mind is "the rules mind with
  the same objective and learned relief estimates"; the rules mind is what a
  learned mind is measured against (the lab's oracle gap) and the fallback
  for far NPCs (item 7). Backends stop writing rule_scores; they supply
  features and needs. Keep backend rule_scores as a legacy shim until the
  goldens (tests/data/inn_rules_*.jsonl, voxel mindlink scenes) are re-pinned
  as declared diffs.
Cost and risk
  Medium. Risk: it is a behaviour change. Declared diffs for every rooms and
  mindlink golden; do it behind a flag, off by default, as the lab does.
  Risk 2: the voxel scenes (duel, fire) were tuned around the hand table
  (mindlink.py:140-148 describes a decision it took to keep a defender from
  walking out a third of the time). Re-tune through needs, not constants.
Status: NEW. Priority: P2. Technical (it implements "no trait flags").

ITEM 9. THE MENU CUT AND THE RULE VETO; THE LEARNED MIND SEES THE FULL MENU
What exists
  core.py:152-221 menu(): pre-rank cut to 16 by max(prior, prior_avg) +
  habit (mass 0.95, 12 first, plus the null act and one of each verb);
  loop.py:206 `player=player or c.batch is not None` is the existing escape
  (a player's menu is never cut, tournament merges); will.py MENU_CAP 7 per
  limb (policy.py:351) kept for non-batchable policies (scripted scenes and
  the mind link: switches.py:520).
Problem
  The cut was designed for a text model that cannot read more than 16
  options (lab: 62 percent right at 16, 47 percent at 64). A learned mind
  scores a long menu in one numpy pass; cutting by the RULES' score hands the
  rules a veto over what the learned mind can ever choose, which contradicts
  "no gate hides an option" (M12, mindlink.py:70-80) and the action scope
  hierarchy principle (characters may choose anything).
Better option
  A learned or rules mind gets the full legal menu (like Player). Cuts and
  batches stay only for text-model choosers (and then only in offline
  evaluation, item 12). For very long menus (16a primitives: body part x
  target x how hard) use the scope hierarchy (scope.py), scoring a scope by
  the log-sum-exp of its members' scores, so the learned mind chooses a
  scope then an act with the same learned values at both levels, no rule
  cut. The `batchable` purity attribute and the tourney path then apply to
  text models only.
Cost: small. Risk: menu size grows in the voxel inn; measure p99 ms (item 7).
Status: NEW (it applies action-scope-hierarchy to the learned mind).
Priority: P2. Technical.

ITEM 10. LEARNED STORES VERSUS TYPED BELIEFS (K1 for the learned mind)
What exists
  api.py:106 Belief(kind, about, value, text, source, how, t): a VIEW, built
  per decision. The voxel store is Mind.bel[key] = (value, ev_id, how, tick),
  at most 64 keys (beliefs.py; BEL_CAP). The rooms backend builds beliefs from
  the inn's rows (backend.py:206-236). K1-K8 extend it to four kinds, seven
  fields (confidence as (r, s) counts, distinct-source count, evidence,
  salience, history, link to the true event), trust per teller, power-law
  forgetting per part. The learned mind keeps its OWN knowledge in
  valence (cue to value), partner models (per person logistic), episodic
  (kNN of steps), concerns, world-model matrices.
Problem
  Two knowledge systems with no bridge. K1 says "records at the boundary,
  learning inside; the learned store reads and writes typed claims at the
  boundary". Today the learned mind's belief that "Tom treats me kindly" is a
  logistic weight no one can read, tell, or cite; and the typed store knows
  nothing the learned stores learned. The cause log (Why: score, margin) says
  nothing of WHY in learned terms. K12 (legibility: why someone believes) is
  therefore impossible for a learned mind.
Better option
  1. A BeliefStore protocol in src/mind (observe(percept), recall(who, query,
     t), tell/hear, forget(t)), the typed table first (K1 start), behind the
     Backend or the Mind. Backends stop building beliefs ad hoc.
  2. PROJECTION: every learned store exposes read-only typed claims of kind
     "who is who" and "how the world works" (for example "act X by partner
     P tends to relieve my need N: confidence from window counts") through
     the same Belief type, so a learned mind can TELL what it learned and the
     cause log can show it. The learned store is the SOURCE OF TRUTH for the
     numbers; the Belief is its report. Never the other way (no writing a
     claim into the learned store by decree except through the cultural
     prior hook, set_prior, already built).
  3. explain(decision) returns the decomposition (actor, habit, valence,
     world, partner, concern, episodic, curiosity: mind.py:220-308 already
     computes these as a dict `last["terms"]`) as typed terms into Why; this
     is "LM and learner output always logged as typed deltas"
     (engine-redesign-target) applied to learning.
Cost: store protocol small; projection per module moderate; K waits for 16b.
Do the explain() part in item 1 (cheap, high value). Status: K1/K12 scheduled
for 16b; the learned-store projection and Why terms are NEW.
Priority: P2 (explain with item 1, projection with 16b). Technical.

ITEM 11. TIME, SLEEP AND THE NIGHT: LEARNING BOUNDARIES MUST BE WORLD EVENTS
What exists
  lab Mind: learn per step, replay at night_end (mind.py:278-292), habit
  fade per night, traces carried over nights (cfg traces). Inn: 300 turns a
  night is the loop. effects/partners windows count turns.
Problem
  In a continuous world there is no "night end" call. Consolidation must
  follow a real sleep job of that character (it can be interrupted, short,
  absent for a sleepless week); step boundaries must follow job ends and
  interrupts, not turns. Reward over a step must integrate over world time
  (a 6-hour sleep is one step of dt 6 h, or the credit window handles it).
Better option
  close(outcome, span_s) and sleep(span_s, depth) driven by the backend from
  its own events (job done or interrupted, `sleeps` event). Habit fade and
  replay scale with the slept span and sleep depth. A character that does not
  sleep does not consolidate (a genuine, testable effect: fatigue degrades
  learning, from the body clock when it exists). Constants in seconds (item 2).
Cost: small once item 2 has the unit change. Status: NEW. Priority: P2.
Technical.

ITEM 12. WHERE A LANGUAGE MODEL MAY BE USED, AND WHERE IT MUST NOT
What exists
  The latency invariant (memory: 6 GB VRAM; a round trip is 13-16 s; reactive
  = zero NPC calls; allowed: the player parse, the addressed NPC's reply).
  src/mind: modes reactive | featured | all (loop.py:82); `featured`: the model
  chooses for named characters and for whoever the player addressed
  (loop.py:180-187 chooser(), Mind.addressed); `all` for offline evaluation;
  Metered counts requests per tick (loop.py:117-141, the only ledger);
  Combined/Gate model choosers pick an OPTION from texts; src/mind/record.py
  records, a lab script scores, Replay applies; no live socket.
  src/core/llm.py ChoiceLM (Qwen3-0.6B) belongs to the dormant engine.
  PA2(b): player free text becomes primitives within the fixed language,
  refused when the target is not perceived. K10: the player sees only what
  the character perceives. MC7: prose by default, numbers when asked.
Problem
  1. The invariant is enforced by structure, not by a test: nothing fails if
     a new feature adds a per-NPC call. Metered counts only the chooser path.
  2. `featured` mode lets a model PICK an NPC's act. That is the wrong job
     for the model that is allowed (the addressed NPC's reply): what a
     character does is the mind's, how it is worded is not. It also keeps
     ~1,100 lines (Combined, Gate, Tourney, the model prompt shape,
     describe text) on the runtime path for a mode the laptop cannot afford.
  3. Three places want a model (parse, reply wording, narration) and the
     budget names two. Narration was never given a rule.
  4. Nothing says what the model's output is allowed to be, so a reply
     could carry facts nobody adjudicated.
Rule proposed (a boundary, with its reason in each line)
  MAY USE A MODEL:
    P1. PLAYER PARSE: free text to a typed primitive sequence, constrained
        to the perceived menu and the closed language (PA2b: refused if the
        target is not perceived). Grammar- or pointer-constrained decoding
        so it cannot invent a target. Pre-parser (keywords, templates)
        first: most turns are cheap imperatives (the latency memory's next
        lever). Fall back to "I did not understand" with the legal menu,
        never to a guess.
    P2. ADDRESSED-NPC REPLY WORDING: the NPC's mind (rules or learned)
        CHOOSES a typed speech act (verb: tell, refuse, ask, greet, lie;
        claim from its own beliefs; stance). The model only WORDS it, given
        the typed act, the character's perceivable voice dials and recent
        lines. Its prose is for the PLAYER's eyes and is never parsed back
        into the world: any NPC that hears the speech hears the typed claim
        (through the sound model, K3), not the prose. Therefore the LM can
        never smuggle a fact, a promise or a deal into the sim.
    P3. OPTIONAL: narration polish (a template description is the default,
        describe.py exists; K10, MC7). One call per PLAYER turn at most,
        only while the player is waiting, off the critical path of the world
        tick, droppable.
    P4. OFFLINE ONLY (never in play): authoring content (data engine),
        generating training data, offline evaluation of model choosers,
        judging. These are the trained-DM direction's uses.
  MUST NOT USE A MODEL:
    N1. any NPC decision in reactive play (the invariant); `featured` and
        `all` are offline evaluation modes, not play modes.
    N2. perception, belief formation, believing, forgetting (K5-K8 are
        processes; the model would be a god-channel).
    N3. adjudication, physics, any change of the world except through the
        validator as typed records (engine-redesign-target: model output is
        logged as typed deltas).
    N4. NPC-to-NPC speech (typed claims only).
    N5. per-tick work of any kind, and learning updates.
    N6. runtime plan search by NPCs (a cached plan may be adopted; filling
        the cache for an off-menu PLAYER intent may call a model once;
        PA4/16b).
  The model service is one engine-wide object: load once, a call budget per
  purpose, a time-out, a fallback (template text), every call logged with
  its purpose and its output; the latency ledger counts calls by purpose per
  turn and the conformance suite asserts zero for NPC decisions.
Better options (code shape)
  1. Replace `Metered` by an engine-wide LM LEDGER (purpose, tokens, ms,
     fallback), asserted in tests (item 6.4).
  2. Move Combined, Gate, Tourney and the featured/all modes to a
     src/mind/eval sub-package; the play loop imports none of it. They stay
     tested (the text-model line of work, SMALL_MODEL_*), since the
     trained-DM direction needs them; but they are not part of the mind
     interface.
  3. Add two small typed interfaces: Parse (text, perceived menu) -> typed
     primitives | refusal, and Voice (typed speech act, voice dials) -> prose,
     each with a deterministic template implementation and a model
     implementation behind the same signature.
Cost: ledger and package move small; Parse and Voice interfaces are the play
loop's (lane C); the mind side is only the typed speech act and the rule.
Risk: narration quality with templates only is low; the owner must say
whether a third call is acceptable (see OWNER CHOICES).
Status: the invariant and PA2 are decided; the boundary rule, the Voice
split, the ledger, demoting featured/all, and the narration rule are NEW.
Priority: P1 for the written boundary and the ledger (cheap, protects the
budget); P2 for the package move; Parse/Voice with the play loop. Owner-level
for narration and for whether `featured` survives; the rest technical.

ITEM 13. THE PLAYER'S CHARACTER IS A MIND TOO (autopilot)
What exists

    character (the mind component) until the gate passes, so a household can
    mix rules and learned minds and the difference is visible.
