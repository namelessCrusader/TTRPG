THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERDICT
  The body is the most realistic part of the engine where a LAW exists (Pennes heat, Purser smoke dose, bone beam check, fuse-chain falls, vessel
  bleeding, XcoM balance, three-compartment fatigue). Its early choices are in the SHAPE of the thing those laws act on and in what has no law at all:
    1. The body is 11 rigid blocks of 5 cm voxels, turned by re-rasterising cells. It has no neck, spine, hand, foot or finger and no flesh stiffness.
    2. The default person is still the 43.9 kg stick figure, and several constants are fudged to suit it.
    3. Walking is a whole-body hop at two fixed speeds.
    4. Consciousness and death are ONE scalar (o2) with fixed lines; there is no heart, so no shock, no pulse, no fainting on standing.
    5. Sight has no light (every room is lit) and is a yes/no threshold; hearing is one level and one 40 dB line.
    6. There is no pharmacology at all (the `drunk` seam is set by nothing).
  The same lesson the owner just drew for trees and planks applies to bodies: the rigid shortcut erased how stiff things are (C1, C4, C5).
  And the cure is the SAME primitive: one articulated rod skeleton with joint and bending stiffness, used for tree, plank, bow and bone alike.

A. SHAPE: HOW THE BODY IS BUILT AND POSED
======================================================================================================================================

C1. THE BODY IS ELEVEN RIGID NAMED VOXEL GROUPS
  CHOICE      scenes.py:514-690 (_person): left/right shin, thigh, upper arm, forearm, torso, head = 11 "bones"; chain, joints, parent dicts at
              scenes.py:620-677. The trunk is ONE bone with a single "lean" hinge at the hips (scenes.py:670-684, lean_max 0.8 rad, materials.py:359
              waist_wmax). The hand rides in the forearm and the foot in the shin (anatomy.py:21); the neck rides in the head. A limb swings as ONE
              rigid thing with the elbow locked (pose.py:30-34).
  WHY         The lattice has no notion of a joint, so the scene that builds the body declares the cells (scenes.py:540-548). Eleven blocks was the
              cheapest thing that gives a knee, an elbow, a lean and a crouch (scenes.py:555-566).
  COSTS       (v) No neck: a head cannot turn, nod or be struck at the chin against the trunk. (v) No spine curve: the trunk cannot twist, so no
              rotation of the hips and shoulders against each other, no rolling over, no curling up, no bent-over carrying with a rounded back, no
              crawl or hands-and-knees gait. (v) No shoulder girdle: no shrug, no reaching across with the shoulder, no hanging by an arm (the
              scapula is what lets the arm go over the head). (v) No ankle or wrist: pose.py:365-367 says "the wrist is a joint whether or not this
              sim models one"; so no heel-to-toe roll, no tiptoe, no wrist bend while holding a tool (a held thing hangs from the fist as a stand-in).
              (v) No pelvis: the hips are the bottom of the torso block. (v) Spine fracture, whiplash and neck injury exist only as table rows
              (anatomy.py MEMBERS), not as motion of a spine.
  BETTER      One ARTICULATED RIGID-BODY SKELETON in reduced coordinates (Featherstone 2008, articulated-body algorithm, O(n) in joints (k)): about
              18-22 segments (head, neck, thorax, abdomen, pelvis, two each of clavicle/scapula, upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shank, foot), segment
              masses and radii of gyration from Winter 2009 / de Leva 1996 / Dempster 1955 (k; bodysheet.py already holds Winter's fractions,
              bodysheet.py:25-30 (v)), joint ranges from clinical ROM tables (Roaas and Andersson 1982; AAOS (k)). The lattice voxels become a SKIN
              drawn from the skeleton each tick (see C2), as characters are skinned in graphics (linear blend skinning, Lewis 2000; dual quaternion
              skinning, Kavan 2007 (k)).
              SAME PRIMITIVE AS THE TREE DECISION: a rod/joint chain with stiffness and a break moment (matbeam.py is the beam check). A bone is a stiff
              rod, a joint is a spring-damper with a stop; a branch is a soft rod. One primitive, many users: that is the "small set of primitives".
  COST+RISK   ~20 joints x 1 body is microseconds per tick in numba or Rust (u). The risk is not the cost but the COUPLING: pose.py (1288 lines),
              skeleton.py, hands.py, bodycontact.py, impact.py and the support law read the voxel body today (p["segs"], _limb_parts). The migration
              needs a seam: skeleton owns the pose, the lattice is derived. Do it behind the SAME interface per the owner's standing rule.
  SCHEDULED?  NOT scheduled. Wave 16a ("six motor primitives" with a BODY PART as the actor) will want hands and feet as parts and will hit this wall.
              Decision to settle before 16a builds on it.
  PRIORITY    HIGH for hands, feet, neck and spine; the rest MEDIUM.
  OWNER-LEVEL YES: it is a representation change (skeleton owns pose; voxels are a view) and it touches the "voxel is the truth" principle.

C2. THE POSE IS MADE BY RE-RASTERISING VOXELS AT 5 CM
  CHOICE      pose.py:_swing (turns a cell set about a pivot, sweeps at 0.08 rad, pose.py:6-9); _repose; the knee crease is one cell wide
              (pose.py:1078-1146). Limbs are two voxels (10 cm) thick (scenes.py:586-609).
  WHY         "the renderer reads mat like everything else": a pose that only changed the picture would have no picture to change (pose.py:22-24).
              That is sound: matter in the lattice is the truth, so a swinging arm really blocks what it occupies.
  COSTS       (v) Angles are quantised by what the lattice can draw ("27 of 31 angles" for a 2-voxel arm; one voxel thick held 4; pose.py comments
              at scenes.py:593-598). (v) Limb sections are 10 cm squares instead of round 8-12 cm limbs; shoulders 35 cm; mass 43.9 kg for a 1.5 m
              person where a real one is 47 (scenes.py:540-548). (v) Flesh at a knee crease must be thickened to conserve mass (pose.py:1086-1090).
              (v) Voxel rotation is a staircase, so inertia, reach and contact area jitter with angle.
  BETTER      Keep matter authoritative but write the body from CAPSULES (analytic volume per cell) into the existing fractional-fill field: the
              lattice already stores a partial fill (smass vs density, senses.py:35-38 packs a cell by fraction (v); body parts use frac 0.85-0.9,
              scenes.py:583). Exact mass per cell from capsule-cell overlap (mass conserved to rounding by construction), smooth under rotation.
              (Kavan skinning for the surface; exact volume overlap is a cheap per-capsule box test (k).)
  COST+RISK   Rasterising 20 capsules into a few hundred cells per body per pose change; cheaper than the cell-set turn plus its drawability search
              (u). Risk: everything that reads `mat == FLESH` as "this cell is a person" (about every law) must read the fraction; bit-for-bit
              golden hashes change (declared diff).
  SCHEDULED?  NOT scheduled. Natural to do together with C1.
  PRIORITY    LOW alone; MEDIUM with C1 (it is what lets a hand and a finger exist).
  OWNER-LEVEL Technical, bundled with C1.

C3. THE DEFAULT PERSON IS A 43.9 KG STICK FIGURE, AND CONSTANTS ARE FUDGED FOR IT
  CHOICE      scenes.py:514 stock figure; bodysheet.py:30-34 says wave 13 "ships on the STOCK 43.9 kg figure unless the user takes decision D7"
              and only ONE scene builds an adult (grep: scenes.py has one hit for adult male). materials.py:295 strength_N 400; materials.py:279-286
              hurt_J 2500 "scaled to THIS body, which is light"; materials.py:344-348 muscle_Pa 93 kPa "LOW for muscle (about 90 kPa against a real
              300) because this body is a stick figure"; materials.py:349 arm_Nm 60. bodysheet.py:232 strength = MU_FOOT x mass x g, LINEAR in mass.
  WHY         The first scenes were built on the stock body, every wave kept it so old results would not move, D7 was never decided.
  COSTS       (v) The calibration targets (fall LD50 12-15 m, survivors 5.2 m, 3 m lumbar fracture, kick 115 J) are adult numbers, but the stock body
              is 2/3 of an adult, so "calibrated" falls are calibrated on a person lighter than the data. bodysheet.py:33 says the stock figure
              shifts every fracture height by 1-2 m. (v) A fudged muscle stress (93 vs 300 kPa) hides a geometry error inside a physics constant:
              the engine's own principle is no stand-ins that look like laws. (v) Strength linear in mass: real maximal strength scales about as
              mass^(2/3) (cross-section; Jaric 2002; Lietzke/Sinclair-type allometric weightlifting scaling (k)), so heavy characters are too strong
              and light ones, children and small women too weak; and a 10 kg child-sized figure "can be picked up like a cat" (scenes.py:524-527 said
              so about an earlier figure).
  BETTER      (a) Take D7: the adult male and female sheets become the default builder, the stock figure kept as a test fixture only. (b) Derive
              muscle force from physiological cross-section (PCSA) = muscle mass / (density x fibre length), specific tension 25-35 N/cm2 (Zajac
              1989; Narici (k)); muscle mass fraction from the sheet with sex and age (Janssen et al. 2000, MRI, 468 adults (k)). That makes sex and
              age differences (upper body about 40% weaker in women, Miller et al. 1993 (k)) EMERGE from composition rather than a 0.90 factor
              (anatomy.py:191 "the sex factor 0.90 is applied ONCE" (v)). (c) Replace the linear-in-mass strength by area scaling.
  COST+RISK   Zero run cost. Large declared diff: every scene and calibration re-measured (wave-sized, but mechanical). Recalibration of falls and kick
              is a re-run of existing fit_report tools.
  SCHEDULED?  PARTLY: Fam3 builds child sheets (docs/family-design.md); wave 14b exertion makes force depend on the person. NOT the adult default,
              NOT PCSA, NOT allometry.
  PRIORITY    HIGH (zero cost, touches every body number, and the family step needs it anyway).
  OWNER-LEVEL YES (D7 is an open owner decision: every old result moves).

C4. JOINTS ARE HARD STOPS PLUS A DAMPER: NO SOFT-TISSUE STIFFNESS, NO JOINT INJURY
  CHOICE      materials.py:410-420 arm_wmax 15 rad/s, joint_damp 2.2 /s; joint angle limits are the lattice's drawability (pose.py:292, scenes.py);
              lean_max 0.8 rad. Nothing in anatomy.py MEMBERS is a ligament, tendon, capsule or cartilage (anatomy.py:19-32 (v)).
  WHY         Bones break by the beam check (bonestate.py); everything else in a joint was left out.
  COSTS       (v) No sprain, dislocation, torn ligament or meniscus: the commonest real injuries of falls, twists and tackles (ankle sprain is the
              single commonest sports injury (k)). (v) A joint cannot be "driven past its range" by a leverage act (an arm-bar, a wrist lock, a
              kneeling joint-lock), which is half of grappling. (v) No passive stiffness: relaxed limbs do not fall limp under gravity, nor do
              braced ones stiffen (impedance, Hogan 1985 (k)); a drunk or unconscious body lands differently from a braced one in life; here the
              difference is a hand-set factor (BRACE_UNFORESEEN 0.3, (u), decisions D3: "no support"). (v) Falls and hits do not damage joints.
  BETTER      Each joint carries (a) a passive torque-angle curve (exponential toward the end of range; Yoon and Mansour 1982, Riener and Edrich
              1999 (k)), (b) active stiffness from co-contraction (impedance, set by the person's state: relaxed, alert, braced), (c) a ligament
              record like the bone record in bonestate.py: {integrity, laxity} with a failure moment from the same beam/moment rule, so sprain =
              partial integrity loss, dislocation = past the stop with a ligament failed. Healing: HL1's repair law applied to the ligament record.
              This is the SAME rod-and-joint primitive as C1 and as the trees (a joint = a short soft rod).
  COST+RISK   Per joint per tick a closed form; nothing new in the lattice. The risk is data: ligament strengths by joint are in the biomechanics
              literature (Woo; Noyes (k)) but each number needs a source and a (u) until fitted.
  SCHEDULED?  NOT scheduled (HL1 repairs "skin, wounds, muscle, vessels, nerves, burns" and bone, healing-design.md:11-15; no ligament or joint).
  PRIORITY    MEDIUM-HIGH (it is what makes falls, grapples and twisting realistic and it reuses the rod primitive).
  OWNER-LEVEL Technical (it is a missing law, not a trade-off).

C5. MUSCLE IS A FEW SCALARS PER BODY, NOT A POSTURE-DEPENDENT TORQUE
  CHOICE      exertion.py (14b): E = F_muscle x stroke x technique x z x state (exertion.py:1-30 (v)); mechlaw.py:614-620 _strength = one force for
              lifts, hauls, drags, shoves and grips; materials.py:295, 349, 358 arm_Nm 60, back_Nm 200, legs_N 1400 / muscle_Pa; fatigue.py per
              limb group (legs, left arm, right arm, back).
  WHY         "Exertion comes from the body" (decision 6). One force per person is the simplest thing that varies by person and state.
  COSTS       (v) Strength does not depend on POSTURE or on the joint angle: a person lifts the same 40 kg off the floor with a rounded back and with a
              straight one, the same at arm's length and close in; real lifting and pushing strength varies 2-5x with posture (Chaffin and Andersson;
              the University of Michigan 3DSSPP (k)). (v) No torque-angle-velocity relation at the joint (a muscle is weakest at the ends of range and
              when shortening fast; Hill 1938, Anderson et al. 2007 (k)), though pose.py names "Hill" for the swing. (v) The same scalar gates grip, push
              and lift, so a strong-legged weak-armed person (and the reverse) cannot exist. (v) Strength is linear in mass (C3).
  BETTER      One MUSCLE-GROUP row per joint axis: peak torque = PCSA x specific tension x moment arm (C3), times a torque-angle curve (a cosine or
              Gaussian about an optimal angle) and a force-velocity factor (Hill). A posture then fixes the torque it can make, and a lift, a push and a
              grip are all the same inverse question (what force at the hand do these joint torques allow). Training (decisions 6, MC3) moves the
              peak; fatigue (fatigue.py) already scales it. This is the "carrying, lifting: joint torques by posture" row of the realism map.
  COST+RISK   Cheap per act (a few torque evaluations). Needs C1 (joints to hang the torque on) for full effect; a first slice can be done on today's
              11 bones.
  SCHEDULED?  PARTLY: realism-map 3.4 "Carrying, lifting" P2 (effort S); exertion (14b) done; grip (wave 15) done as a hand law. Torque by posture NOT scheduled.
  PRIORITY    MEDIUM.
  OWNER-LEVEL Technical.

C6. A STRIKE'S ENERGY IS "FORCE x STROKE", NOT A KINETIC CHAIN
  CHOICE      exertion.py:KINDS: E = F x stroke; effective mass is a fraction of the body (exertion.py:30-50 (v)). pose.py/_swing moves one limb with
              one shoulder torque.
  WHY         It fits the one published kick number (Ramakrishnan et al. 2018) and costs nothing.
  COSTS       No trunk rotation, hip drive or step into a blow, so "power comes from the legs and hips" is not a thing; a boxer's and a wrestler's
              technique lives only in a scalar. A throw, a sword cut and a punch share one number.
  BETTER      Proximal-to-distal sequencing (Putnam 1993 (k)): segment speeds add along the chain (hips to trunk to arm to hand), each driven by its
              joint torque from C5. Falls out of C1 + C5 for free with an articulated body; not worth doing on its own.
  COST+RISK   With C1: microseconds. Without C1: no.
  SCHEDULED?  NOT scheduled.   PRIORITY LOW (after C1).   OWNER-LEVEL Technical.

C7. THE HAND IS A LUMP OF VOXELS WITH ONE "N_EFF"
  CHOICE      hands.py (reach by king's-move cell distance, hands.py:8-40); grip.py:41 N_EFF = 2.0 opposed pads; materials.py:300 wrist_Nm 20; the hand
              has no cells of its own (it rides in the forearm).
  WHY         A 5 cm cell is bigger than a finger; hands as voxels is impossible, so a number stands in for them.
  COSTS       No fingers: no pick-a-lock dexterity, knot tying, drawing a bowstring (three-finger draw), holding a pen, sewing, archery release,
              sword grip variations, pinching a coin, or a hand with a missing finger or a burned tight hand (HL3 "the burned smith's tight hand"
              needs somewhere to live). Grip law (wave 15) reads "a pair of surfaces and the film" but the contact geometry is the whole hand.
  BETTER      Model the hand at its REAL level as a RECORD, like the bone record: {aperture, pad area per digit, digit strengths, dexterity, ROM,
              injuries}, with the grasp type from the GRASP taxonomy (Feix et al. 2016) / Napier's power and precision grips (1956) (k) as a small enum
              of contact patterns. Force closure and friction on those patterns is a few lines (grip.py already has the friction math). Dexterity is
              a dial trained by use (MC3) and lowered by injury (HL3). The lattice only sees the held object.
  COST+RISK   Tiny. The risk is interface: 16a's GRIP/RELEASE primitive takes "a body part (hand, hands, foot...) and HOW CAREFULLY" (decisions PA1),
              which maps directly onto a hand record with a precision dial.
  SCHEDULED?  PARTLY: 16a GRIP primitive and wave 15 grip law; the hand RECORD is not.
  PRIORITY    MEDIUM (it unlocks craft: lock picking, tying, writing, archery, smithing tongs).
  OWNER-LEVEL YES-ish: "levels" principle (a hand is a record, not voxels) is a scope and play call.

C8. WALKING IS A WHOLE-BODY HOP AT TWO FIXED SPEEDS
  CHOICE      policy.py:75,81 walk_mps 1.4, run_mps 2.5; motion.py:907-970 _gait returns "run" (fleeing) / "walk" / "creep"; _pace converts a speed to ticks
              per voxel, scaled only by strength / (strength - haul) (motion.py:944-949); _stride (motion.py:950+) steps the whole body one voxel at a
              time when a stride counter has paid for it. Legs do not swing; the body is translated.
  WHY         Cheap and exact for routes; speeds were set to the walk-to-run transition (Hreljac 1993).
  COSTS       (v) Speed does not depend on leg length, age, load (except haul), fatigue, slope (only by the stairs/drops branch), ground, a limp is the
              only injury effect (bonestate gait: pace / (0.4 + 0.6 ratio)). A child, an old man, a 2.2 m giant and a fit runner all walk at 1.4 and
              flee at 2.5 m/s. (v) No acceleration: a person starts and stops instantly (realism map "instant starts"), so you cannot "get a head
              start" by a fraction of a second, a sprint finish, or a stop-on-a-dime slip. (v) A run is a speed flag set by `fleeing`, not an effort
              chosen: no jog, no sprint, no pacing for distance (the metabolic cost is billed but the effort level is not a choice). (v) No leg
              animation: others "see" a person move but no gait cycle exists to hear footfalls (impact sounds by contact pair, realism-map 3.5, P2), or
              to carry cadence into the sound model or the stumble model.
  BETTER      Speed as a function of the person: preferred walking speed from leg length (dimensionless speed v/sqrt(g L): Froude 0.25-0.35 for a
              comfortable walk, walk-to-run transition at Fr 0.5; Alexander 1976 (k)), age and sex norms (Bohannon 1997; Bohannon and Williams Andrews
              2011 (k)), cost of transport by gradient (Minetti et al. 2002 (k); Margaria 1963), cadence = f(speed, leg length) (Dean 1965; Grieve (k)).
              A speed controller with a bounded acceleration (a <= mu g at the foot; the realism map's own rule) and a chosen EFFORT (walk, jog, run,
              sprint) as an act parameter (16a "how hard, how fast"). Gait cycle as a timer that drives a foot-contact event stream (heel strikes) from
              which footfall sound, slip and stumble all read. The body can still move as a cell hop in the lattice; what changes is WHEN the hop happens.
  COST+RISK   Small: replace two constants by a function of the sheet plus an acceleration state per walker (per tick scalar). Risk: the goldens for
              every chase and flee scene move (declared diffs); route timing in learned-mind runs changes (lab inn travel times).
  SCHEDULED?  PARTLY: "Gait, speed, stairs ... one rewrite together with route bounding" is realism-map 3.4 P1 effort L. Stairs (2.5-D) were done in wave 13 and
              the stairs hull is wave 15 batch 3; the SPEED law and acceleration are not named in any decision.
  PRIORITY    HIGH (who outruns whom is the core of every chase, flee and fear scene, and children and the old are everywhere in the family step).
  OWNER-LEVEL Technical (the numbers are measured).

C9. ROUTES ARE ON AN 8-NEIGHBOUR GRID (OCTILE METRIC)
  CHOICE      motion.py:21-23 _STEPS8: costs 70 and 99 (1 : 1.414), no any-angle moves; headings are 8 directions and gaze sweeps 8 headings (policy.py:15
              GAZE_SWEEP).
  WHY         Cheap exact A*; reproducible ties ("grid steps first").
  COSTS       Octile distance overstates a straight line by up to 8% off the axes; a body walks along stair-stepped lines and turns only at 45 degrees. The
              fix "a room at 45 degrees walked 1.41 times as far" (motion.py:97 (v)) shows this bit before. Corner cutting around door frames is
              grid-shaped; people never "round a corner smoothly".
  BETTER      Any-angle paths (Theta*, Daniel et al. 2010; Lazy Theta* (k)) with the same legality test, a continuous heading, and a turn-rate limit by speed
              (v^2/r <= mu g; realism map). Keep the grid as the legality structure.
  COST+RISK   Moderate (line-of-sight checks); exactness rules (the sim carries no die) need a deterministic tiebreak. Risk: route goldens.
  SCHEDULED?  NOT scheduled (the realism map ties it to "route bounding").   PRIORITY LOW.   OWNER-LEVEL Technical.

C10. BALANCE: ONE STEP-OR-GRAB REFLEX ON A QUASI-STATIC BODY
  CHOICE      motion.py:541-770 hull of weight-bearing contacts, _xcom (Hof 2005) at motion.py:637; reflex.py:91 balance row (delay 90-120 ms);
              bodycontact.py 29-37 XcoM with an ankle-torque limit (ankle_cop_m); Hof counter-rotation (14b E).
  WHY         The margin of stability against the hull is the standard test and it is cheap; the reflex only runs "when the margin is small".
  COSTS       (v) A walking person is always balanced unless hit or slipping; there is no gait-phase balance (single support, swing leg). (v) One
              recovery rule: step to catch, or grab; no ankle strategy for small pushes, no hip strategy for medium, no deliberate step order (Horak and
              Nashner 1986 (k)), no trip response (swing-foot collision, the commonest cause of elderly falls: Pavol 2001 (k)). (v) Slip reaction exists
              via friction (14b H) but no sensory noise: no vestibular threshold, no proprioceptive loss with age, no dizziness; the balance reflex
              does not degrade except by drunk/fatigue/sleep_debt factors that "nothing sets" (reflex.py:24-28).
  BETTER      Keep the XcoM test as the trigger. Add (a) the SWING FOOT as a second contact during walk phases (needs C8's gait timer): trips become
              "a contact on the swing foot", (b) a three-tier response by margin deficit (ankle -> hip -> step), each with a time and cost (Horak and
              Nashner (k)), (c) sensory dials (vision, vestibular, proprioception, each with age and drink) that set the reflex delay and error.
              Pratt 2006 capture point for the step target (k).
  COST+RISK   Small; only runs when the margin is small. Risk: extends reflex.py rows; keep the one contract.
  SCHEDULED?  PARTLY: 14b E (Hof counter-rotation, ankle limit) and H (friction); D3 fall preparation in wave 15 b2. Strategies and trips NOT scheduled.
  PRIORITY    MEDIUM.   OWNER-LEVEL Technical.

C11. FALL INJURY: A 1-D FUSE CHAIN WITH MODE TABLES TUNED TO TWO POPULATION NUMBERS
  CHOICE      impact.py:1-45 (series elements with capacity, stroke and failure; closed form in three stages; "mode tables ... are DATA. If the random-
              orientation targets are not met (survivors average 5.2 m, LD50 12-15 m), the table is retuned, not the law", (v)).
  WHY         It is exact, cheap and every joule has a sink (impact.py:25-30); the calibration targets are the best published population data.
  COSTS       (v) Orientation matters only through discrete modes (feet, side, back, shoulder, head, arms), so rolling, arm-out, a hand-first fall and a
              slide are not separate physics (Hof's counter-rotation covers part). Tuning two population numbers can hide a wrong mode share.
  BETTER      Use the offline MULTIBODY as a REFERENCE (owner Q14 (b), "an offline controller used ONLY as a reference test target"): a rigid multibody
              (MuJoCo or Featherstone code in the lab) drops a C1-style skeleton at several orientations and speeds; compare peak forces per region
              with the fuse chain and the mode shares. Keeps the sim exact and cheap and tests it against a physics that has orientation.
              Fall preparation (D3, wave 15) grows with warning time; hand impact at 680 ms (decisions line 246) is a source.
  COST+RISK   Lab time only; no run cost.   SCHEDULED? Owner Q14 (b) says a LOW-priority lab experiment. Fine as is.
  PRIORITY    LOW.   OWNER-LEVEL No (already decided).

C12. NO HEART: BLOOD PRESSURE IS A STRAIGHT LINE IN BLOOD VOLUME
  CHOICE      vasculature.py:8-12: MAP_rel(f) = clip((f - 0.25)/(0.80 - 0.25), 0, 1), MAP_0 12 kPa; perfusion to the brain clip((f-0.45)/0.55)^0.56;
              faint at 36 % lost, death at 50.4 % lost, "so these lines do not move" (vasculature.py:10-11 (v)). Heart rate does not exist.
  WHY         The cut-vessel law needs a driving pressure; a linear map from volume fraction is the minimum that makes bleeding slow as blood drains.

  CHOICE      bodysheet.py: sheets ADULT_MALE/ADULT_FEMALE/STOCK as fixed dicts; anatomy.py:191 sex factor 0.90 on top of size; "age_y" used only for a
              bone threshold (bodysheet.py:101 AGE_BONE_START_Y, :218) and bmr; Fam5 puts the full life course LATER.
  WHY         A fixed household is enough for the inn (Fam5 (c)).
  COSTS       Children are not smaller adults: head proportion, mass fraction in the legs, surface area per mass (a child cools twice as fast), growth of bone,
              muscle, and heart. Ageing: sarcopenia, lower VO2 max, slower reflexes, loss of bone (C16), reduced thermoregulation, hearing (C25),
              vision (C23). Women's dimorphism as one 0.90 factor misses mass, body fat, muscle distribution (C3).
  BETTER      Growth curves from anthropometric tables (CDC/WHO growth charts, Preece-Baines (k)) as a function that generates a sheet at age a (geometry and
              composition); ageing as slow drifts of the same dials (muscle_scale, bone_scale, reflex band, acuity, hearing threshold, set points), each
              a measured trajectory (Janssen 2000; Fleg 2005 for VO2max (k)). One generator `sheet(age, sex, genome)` that feeds C3.
  COST+RISK   Data and one function; zero run cost.
  SCHEDULED?  PARTLY: Fam3 builds child sheets; Fam5 (c) says the FULL life course comes later with genetics. Adult/old trajectories not named.
  PRIORITY    LOW until the family step; then MEDIUM.   OWNER-LEVEL Technical.
