0. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER
======================================================================
Adding one coupling (M couples to information-bearing structure) is consistent only if five things come with it:
 a) M has its own dynamics (it moves, spreads, decays, is used up), so "action at a distance" is really "action through a field at finite speed".
 b) The coupling runs both ways (Onsager reciprocity): whatever can send M can also sense it, and recoil exists.
 c) M must be OUT of equilibrium with matter. A field in thermal equilibrium with matter gives no work (second law). So M is a battery with a gradient, not a free lunch.
 d) Every release is bounded by a booked store: caster body, local M, or a charged material. Small trigger, big release is lawful only because a metastable store already holds the energy.
 e) Gain above 1 (feedback) is allowed, but it is capped by the store, like a laser: threshold, then saturation.
Landauer, Szilard and Sagawa-Ueda give the real link between information and energy: a pattern can pay for or earn energy at a rate set by bits times kT ln2. That is tiny (3e-21 J per bit at 300 K), so "pattern transduces M" is lawful, but the pattern is a CONTROLLER (steers a flow), not a power source. This matches the user's rule: energy comes from the store, the pattern only decides where it goes.

1. FIELDS AND WAVES: WHAT PROPERTIES SHOULD M HAVE
======================================================================
Real behaviour (to copy as forms):

1.1 Speed. Waves in a medium have phase speed v = w/k and group speed vg = dw/dk. Light in vacuum 3.0e8 m/s; sound in air 343 m/s, water 1480, steel ~5900; seismic P-waves ~6 km/s. Dispersion (v depends on frequency) makes a pulse spread: a pattern that is a bundle of frequencies smears with distance. For an information-carrying pulse of bandwidth B over distance L the spread is about L*(dvg/dw)*B (u, standard dispersion result). Consequence for M: with dispersion, complex (wide-band) spells degrade with range; simple narrow-band ones carry far. This gives a lawful range-versus-complexity trade without a special rule.

1.2 Attenuation. Beer-Lambert: I(x) = I0 * exp(-mu x). Add geometric spreading: spherical 1/r^2, cylindrical 1/r, beam ~constant out to Rayleigh range. Seawater kills radio: skin depth about 8 m at 1 kHz (delta ~ 503*sqrt(rho/(mu_r f)) m, rho = 0.25 ohm-m). Data: mu per material per band.

1.3 Reflection and absorption at boundaries. Normal incidence power reflection R = ((Z2 - Z1)/(Z2 + Z1))^2, where Z is wave impedance (acoustic Z = rho*v; electromagnetic Z = sqrt(mu/eps)). Water to air for sound: R ~ 99.9%. Matching layers (quarter-wave, thickness lambda/4n) give zero reflection at one wavelength: the real basis of anti-reflection coatings. Parameter: each material gets an M-impedance Z_M and absorption mu_M. A "mirror" is a big impedance step; a "shield" is large mu_M; an "absorber" is matched Z with large mu_M.

1.4 Shielding. Faraday cage: a conductor of thickness more than a few skin depths blocks EM fields. Skin depth delta = sqrt(2 rho / (w mu)). Copper at 60 Hz: 8.5 mm; at 1 MHz: 66 micrometres; at 1 GHz: 2 micrometres. Shielding effectiveness about exp(-t/delta) (absorption part), ~8.7 dB per skin depth. A mesh with holes smaller than lambda/10 works at wavelength lambda (u, rule of thumb): holes larger than the wavelength leak. Static magnetic fields need high-permeability alloys (mu-metal) that redirect flux, not absorb it. Lessons for M: shielding is frequency-dependent (a cage tuned to one band leaks in another), and a shield made of a conductor-like "M-conductor" reflects while a lossy one absorbs and heats (absorbed energy becomes heat in the shield, booked).

1.5 Interference. Two waves of amplitude A1, A2 give intensity I = I1 + I2 + 2 sqrt(I1 I2) cos(phase difference). Full cancellation needs equal amplitude and opposite phase. Active noise cancellation (Lueg patent 1936; headphones commercial from 1980s) works only for low frequency (below ~1 kHz) and only in a small zone (zone of quiet ~ lambda/10 in size, u). Lesson: a counterspell by interference is lawful but local, narrow-band, and needs phase knowledge of the incoming wave. It does not delete the energy: the sum of intensities is redistributed (energy goes to the places of constructive interference, or into the canceller's source work). Booked, no loss of conservation.

1.6 Resonance and standing waves. Driven oscillator: amplitude peaks at the natural frequency, width = f0/Q. Stored energy builds up to about Q times the drive per cycle energy. Q values: tuning fork ~1e3 (u), quartz crystal 1e4 to 1e6, superconducting RF cavity 1e10 (u). Standing wave in a cavity of length L: L = n*lambda/2. Laser and maser (Townes 1954; Schawlow and Townes 1958; Einstein 1917 for stimulated emission) are the best template: a gain medium with population inversion (stored, metastable energy) plus a resonant cavity (a pattern in the geometry) plus a trigger gives a coherent, narrow, intense output, with a threshold and then saturation. This is exactly "M = population inversion; engraving = cavity; trigger = pump or seed".

1.7 Focusing. Diffraction limit: smallest spot about lambda/(2 NA) (Abbe 1873); Gaussian beam waist w0 and Rayleigh range zR = pi w0^2 / lambda. So a spot of size s can be held only over a depth ~ s^2/lambda. Fine aim and long range are in tension: precision costs short wavelength (and so higher photon-like quantum energy, more absorption). Lenses and mirrors (engraved shapers) work with focal ratio set by size over wavelength. Intensity gain of a focusing aperture: area/spot area.

1.8 Near and far field. Reactive near field is r < lambda/(2 pi): energy is stored and returned to the source, not radiated (the basis of inductive wireless charging, Kurs et al., Science 2007: 60 W over 2 m with resonant coupling at 10 MHz, ~40% efficiency). Far field starts around r > 2 D^2/lambda (Fraunhofer distance). Lesson: a spell cast at touch range can be strong and efficient by near-field coupling (no radiation, little waste); long range means radiative far field with 1/r^2 loss. Touch spells vs ranged spells fall out of the field, not rules.

What properties should M have? Proposed form (all data):
 - M(x,t) scalar density field (units: joule of "convertible energy" per cubic metre), not a vector. Energy-like, so the ledger stays in joules.
 - Slow bulk dynamics: dM/dt = D laplacian(M) + S(x) - kM - u(x,t); D diffusion (spreading), S source (ley, refill), k leak (decay to heat, booked), u use by structures. This gives "finite, depletes where used, refills slowly, places differ".
 - Fast pulse channel: a wave mode on top of the density, with speed c_M (pack parameter, can be 1 m/s to near-instant), dispersion exponent, attenuation mu_M per material, impedance Z_M per material. Waves carry energy and momentum density. Casting emits a wave packet; the effect arrives at c_M ("taking hold is information-fast").
 - Wavelength set by the pattern's feature size (lambda_M = c_M / f), so fine patterns mean short wavelength and the diffraction limit applies.
 - Quantisation not needed. A minimum useful packet size is optional (a pack could require a threshold, like a laser threshold).

2. METASTABILITY AND TRIGGERS: SMALL PUSH, BIG RELEASE, BOUNDED
======================================================================
General law: a system sits in a local minimum of free energy with barrier Ea above it and a lower state below by dE. A trigger supplies at least Ea (or just seeds a nucleus); the release is dE per unit mass times the mass converted. Gain G = release / trigger can be 1e6 to 1e12, but total release is hard-capped by stored dE times mass. Nothing is created.

Real cases:
 - Hydrocarbon-air ignition: minimum ignition energy about 0.2 mJ (hydrogen 0.017 mJ, u for exact values). Fuel burns release ~46 MJ/kg (gasoline), so 1 g of fuel releases 46 kJ from a 0.2 mJ spark: gain ~2e8.
 - Explosives: TNT 4.2 MJ/kg, detonation speed ~6.9 km/s; ANFO ~3.7 MJ/kg; black powder ~3 MJ/kg; gasoline-air mixtures per kg of mixture are smaller. Detonation is a shock-supported reaction front (Chapman 1899, Jouguet 1905, Zeldovich, von Neumann, Doering 1940s: ZND model). Deflagration (subsonic flame) vs detonation (supersonic) is a regime change set by confinement and fuel reactivity.
 - Thermal explosion theory (Semenov 1928; Frank-Kamenetskii 1939): a reactive body of size R ignites when heat generation exceeds loss, i.e. when the dimensionless Frank-Kamenetskii number delta = (Q A E R^2 e^(-E/RT0))/(lambda_th R_gas T0^2) exceeds a critical value (0.88 for a slab, 2.0 for a sphere, 3.32 for an infinite cylinder; u for exact values). Lesson: there is a critical size; a small pile of oil rags is stable, a large one self-heats. This is a general law already compatible with the sim's combustion.
 - Supercooled water: pure small droplets stay liquid to about -38 C (homogeneous nucleation, Pruppacher and Klett). Sodium acetate hand warmers: latent heat ~265 kJ/kg (u), triggered by a clicking metal disc that makes nucleation sites.
 - Superheated liquids: bubble chamber (Glaser 1952, Nobel 1960): a liquid held just under boiling is triggered by the ionisation of one passing charged particle (keV deposited) into a visible bubble track. Cloud chamber (C.T.R. Wilson 1911) does the same for supersaturated vapour.
 - Classical nucleation theory: rate J = J0 exp(-dG*/(kT)); dG* = 16 pi gamma^3 / (3 (dGv)^2). The rate is extremely steep in the driving force: a small extra push changes the rate by orders of magnitude. This is why triggers look like switches.
 - Avalanche and chain reaction: if each event triggers k others and k > 1, growth is exponential until the store is gone (Fermi CP-1 reactor 1942: k from <1 to 1.0006). Self-organised criticality (Bak, Tang, Wiesenfeld 1987): sandpiles relax in avalanches with power-law sizes, many small few large, driven slowly. Real avalanches: snow slabs release stored gravitational energy with a tiny trigger; the energy is the slab, not the skier.
 - Phase transitions: Landau theory (1937) treats the order parameter near a transition; first-order transitions show hysteresis (metastable range) and latent heat; second-order are continuous.

How they are bounded (all lawful, all booked):
 1. Energy cap: release <= dE_store x mass. 2. Barrier: the trigger energy must exceed Ea or hit a nucleation site; below it, nothing happens (no partial credit). 3. Spatial: a front moves at a finite speed (flame 0.4 m/s for methane-air; detonation km/s). 4. Dissipation: a chain with k < 1 dies. 5. Recovery: the store must be recharged (the sim can book the recharge as an input).

Implication for magic: "a spark that lights a cask" is already lawful in the sim. A magic trigger can be defined as: a pattern whose output is a nucleus or an ignition event aimed at a metastable store (fuel, supercooled water, a charged crystal, a loaded M-pocket). The cleverer caster is the one who knows where metastable stores sit. A CHARGED crystal is a metastable M store: energy density parameter (J/kg), barrier Ea (J), leak rate (half-life), trigger site. Compare real storage: Li-ion ~0.9 MJ/kg, gasoline 46, hydrogen 120, TNT 4.2, flywheel steel ~0.1, supercap ~0.03 MJ/kg (u). A "crystal" at 5 MJ/kg would be pack-tunable as rarity.

3. SELF-SUSTAINING PATTERNS: SPIRITS, PATRONS, GODS
======================================================================
Real families (all are non-equilibrium; none persists for free):

 - Solitons: Scott Russell 1834 (canal wave that kept shape for miles); Korteweg and de Vries 1895 equation u_t + 6 u u_x + u_xxx = 0; Zabusky and Kruskal 1965 showed solitons pass through each other unchanged. Balance of nonlinearity and dispersion keeps the shape. Optical solitons in fibre: Hasegawa and Tappert 1973; used in telecom. They persist as long as loss is low: real fibre loss 0.2 dB/km, so they need amplifiers. Solitons carry energy and momentum and are lossless only in ideal media.
 - Vortex rings: Helmholtz 1858 (vorticity lines move with the fluid; closed rings persist), Kelvin 1867 (vortex atom idea). Smoke rings last seconds, decay by viscosity. In a superfluid, circulation is quantised (Onsager 1949, Feynman 1955) and persistent currents last essentially forever (u: experiments suggest more than 1e5 years extrapolated). Topological protection: a pattern that cannot unwind without a large barrier survives with no ongoing energy.
 - Turing patterns: Turing 1952 showed that a fast-diffusing inhibitor and a slow activator make spots and stripes spontaneously. Gray-Scott model (1984), Pearson 1993 (Science, "Complex patterns in a simple system") gives self-replicating spots that split and fill space, fed by a continuous inflow of one chemical. Spots die if the feed stops.
 - Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction: Belousov 1951 (published 1959), Zhabotinsky 1964, Oregonator model Field and Noyes 1974. Chemical oscillations and travelling spiral waves, sustained while reagents last, then it dies.
 - Dissipative structures: Prigogine (Nobel 1977): ordered structures sustained by a flow of energy and the export of entropy. Schrodinger 1944: life feeds on low entropy. England 2013 (J Chem Phys, "Statistical physics of self-replication") derived a lower bound for the heat a self-replicator must dissipate in terms of growth rate, decay rate and entropy change. Autopoiesis: Maturana and Varela 1972. Cellular examples: Conway's Life 1970 (gliders move forever, but only because the rule is a free engine), Lenia (Chan 2019) shows life-like persistent creatures in continuous cellular space.
 - Physical existence proofs: hurricanes (self-sustaining while warm ocean feeds them, ~1e14 W for a mature storm, u), flames (self-sustaining pattern fed by fuel and oxygen), the Great Red Spot (centuries of persistence, fed by turbulence; u), plasmoids and ball lightning (poorly understood, u).

Could M patterns be spirits, patrons or gods? Yes, as a general category: a dissipative structure in the M field. What it needs:
 (1) A throughput. Intake power >= dissipation + maintenance. Dissipation comes from leak k of the field (M turns to heat) and from error-correction. Intake sources: a steady M source (a ley line), a gradient it harvests, a living body (a feeder, who pays), or a store (a crystal). Minimum power depends on pattern size: a living cell is about 1e-12 W (u); human 100 W basal; a storm 1e14 W. Kleiber 1932: metabolic rate ~ mass^0.75.
 (2) A boundary or a topological defect to keep the pattern from diffusing (a membrane, a vortex, a bound soliton, an engraved vessel).
 (3) Repair: errors accumulate at a rate set by noise; repair must outrun it (error-correcting code threshold, von Neumann 1956; Landauer cost kT ln2 per bit of erased error, tiny but real).
 (4) For minds: enough information bits and processing. Brain uses ~20 W; Landauer limit at 20 W and 300 K would allow 7e21 irreversible bit operations per second, far above the brain's actual ~1e15 synaptic events per second (u), so energy is not the binding limit; the limit is the physical hardware.
So a "patron" or "god" is a pattern with a big, lasting M intake, a channel to its worshippers (the lasting channel), and mind-level information. A "spirit" is a smaller one. Deaths: cut the intake or break the boundary. Dying fades as a decay, not a switch. Ghosts: a pattern with low intake that fades; hauntings correlate with M-rich places.
Minimum "diet" of a spirit is therefore parameters: intake rate (W), leak (1/s), repair threshold, boundary strength. Hungry spirits cluster at strong sources; the sim can compute this with the same ledger.

4. INFORMATION AND ENERGY: DOES "PATTERNS TRANSDUCE M" HAVE A REAL BASIS?
======================================================================
 - Landauer 1961: erasing one bit costs at least kT ln2 of heat. At 300 K that is 2.87e-21 J (0.018 eV). Experimentally verified: Berut et al., Nature 2012 (colloidal bead in a double well, reaching the limit in slow cycles); Jun, Gavrilov and Bechhoefer 2014 PRL (u for the exact venue).
 - Szilard 1929: one molecule, a partition and a measurement of its side gives up to kT ln2 of work. Bennett 1982: the demon's memory must be reset, and that reset pays the cost, so the second law holds.
 - Experiments that turn information into energy: Toyabe et al., Nature Physics 2010 (a particle goes up a spiral staircase of potential, feedback holds it; free energy gained from information, about 28% of kT ln2 per bit for the efficiency reported, u); Koski et al., PNAS 2014 (single-electron Szilard engine, up to ~75% of kT ln2 per cycle, u).
 - Generalised second law: Sagawa and Ueda 2008, 2010 PRL: average extractable work <= -dF + kT * I, where I is the mutual information gained by measurement. Parrondo, Horowitz and Sagawa, Nature Physics 2015, "Thermodynamics of information", review. Jarzynski 1997 equality <exp(-W/kT)> = exp(-dF/kT).
 - Information-processing limits: Margolus and Levitin 1998: at most 2E/(pi hbar) operations per second for energy E; Lloyd 2000 (Nature) "Ultimate physical limits to computation"; Bekenstein bound S <= 2 pi k R E/(hbar c) bits-wise.
Reading these for Mark_1:
 - There is a lawful, quantitative link: information about a system lets you extract at most kT ln2 per bit of work from a thermal reservoir, and storing/erasing patterns costs the same. So an information-bearing structure that measures and feeds back is a lawful Maxwell-demon-style transducer: it extracts work from a gradient. The ENERGY comes from the gradient (M field, thermal gradient, stored charge), the PATTERN selects which gradient, when and where.
 - The bit-scale limit is tiny: to extract 0.9 GJ via bits alone at 300 K needs ~3e29 bits. So pure information-to-work is NOT a source for fireballs; it is a valve. (The user's decision 8.1 is consistent: sources are body, M, charged material.)
 - Real systems operate way above Landauer (a neuron spike costs ~1e-9 J at ~1e4 ATP-ish per spike, about 1e11 times kT ln2, u). So a pack can set "pattern overhead" per bit much larger than kT ln2; the floor is physical, the actual cost is data. Waste heat of casting then follows the book: caster's brain pays overhead, M pays the effect.
 - A good law: transduction power P_out <= eta * (power drawn from source), where the pattern's complexity (bits) sets the control precision and the overhead cost, not the output.
 - Consistent reading for "better knowledge makes a better caster": a caster who knows the gradient (thermal, chemical, metastable) has more mutual information with the world, which by Sagawa-Ueda allows more extractable work per decision. Real physics.

5. THERMODYNAMIC LIMITS ON TRANSDUCTION, AND WASTE HEAT
======================================================================
 - Carnot: eta <= 1 - Tc/Th. 600 K to 300 K: 50%. Real engines 25 to 45% (diesel ~45%, combined cycle plants ~60%). Body temperature 310 K vs 300 K: Carnot only 3%, so a body cannot do heat-engine work from its own heat; muscle runs on chemical energy at ~25% efficiency (Hill 1938 on muscle; u).
 - Endoreversible engines, Curzon and Ahlborn 1975: at maximum power eta = 1 - sqrt(Tc/Th). 600/300: 29%. So fast machines are less efficient: a power-versus-efficiency trade.
 - Photovoltaic: Shockley and Queisser 1961: 33.7% max for a single junction (best silicon cells ~27%; multi-junction concentrators ~47%, u). Photosynthesis: theoretical ~4.6% C3, ~6% C4 (Zhu, Long, Ort 2008); real crops 1 to 2%. Betz 1919: a wind turbine cannot take more than 16/27 = 59.3% of the wind's power. Li-ion round trip 90 to 95%. Electric motor 90 to 97%. Fuel cell ~60%. LEDs 50% to 70% (u).
 - Lasers: electrical to optical 10 to 50% (diode lasers up to ~65%), so the rest is waste heat that must be removed. Gain medium warms; thermal lensing limits power; cooling sets the max.
 - Waste heat law: Q_waste = (1 - eta) * P_in, booked at the transducer. The user's split (work + light + heat = 1) is the same law with light as the optical channel.
 - Every conversion step multiplies: chain efficiency = product. A chain with 5 links at 70% gives 17%. This is the lawful reason "magic that conserves energy with losses at every link" works.
 - Entropy limit: heat can only flow hot to cold unaided; making ice from a warm room (heat pump) needs work. COP_cooling <= Tc/(Th - Tc). Cooling a body from 310 K to 270 K at 300 K outside: COP max 6.75 (u, calc). So "freeze" spells cost work scaling with the temperature gap, plus waste heat dumped nearby: a freeze spell warms the caster's surroundings (booked).
 - Heat removal: the caster's body can lose about 100 to 1000 W (sweating raises this to maybe 1 kW; u) so a spell that wastes 50 kJ at 30% efficiency per second would overheat a human in minutes (body heat capacity ~3.5 kJ/(kg K) x 70 kg = 245 kJ per K; a 2 K rise is 0.5 MJ). So caster waste heat is a natural fatigue limit, with fever and burns, no extra rule needed.

6. THE MINIMAL-CHANGE QUESTION
======================================================================
What does one coupling imply? Write the coupling as an interaction energy density H_int = g * M(x) * I(x), where I(x) is a local "pattern functional" of the structure (a measure of its organised information content, for example the complexity or low-entropy part) and g the coupling. What follows:
 1. Noether (1918): energy conservation comes from time-translation invariance; momentum from space-translation invariance. Add the term to a translation-invariant action and both are still conserved, but the budget now includes M. Hence M MUST carry energy and momentum (a stress-energy). That is why recoil exists, and why a pulse of M exerts force like light pressure (radiation pressure P = I/c).
 2. Reciprocity (Onsager 1931; Casimir 1945 for the magnetic-field case): if the structure can be driven by M, it must also radiate or leak M when driven by something else, and the same coupling constant governs both. A caster is detectable by their casting (the "tell", and M-sensitive creatures work as detectors, antenna theory: a good transmitter is a good receiver). Matches Beast detection, wards, and the visible tell.
 3. Locality and causality. Fields give contact interactions through a medium with finite speed c_M. "At a distance" is acceptable if the distance is covered by a wave. If c_M is huge but finite, effect times are ~instant and everything else (no faster than the speed limit) stays causal. Pack parameter c_M. If c_M = infinity would be required, conservation bookkeeping breaks (no consistent order of events); the sim's tick order would pick an arbitrary frame (u: this is a design issue, not physics). So keep c_M finite: bound it by at least one cell per tick.
 4. Second law and the equilibrium problem. If M were in thermal equilibrium with matter at temperature T, no net work could be extracted (Kelvin-Planck). So M must be a non-equilibrium reservoir: a "temperature" or chemical-potential difference between M and the matter. M-at-zero is the cold, empty case; spent M relaxes into heat (k leak) and the process is irreversible. The refill source must itself be powered (by something booked or declared an external source: sun, ley, planet) and must be finite in rate. A pack that wants a "sealed" world can book the source as a parameter. This makes refill rate and place variation consistent.
 5. Stability against runaway. Feedback with gain > 1 grows exponentially until a saturation (laser threshold and saturation; Einstein 1917). The gain-limiting thing is the local store. So the engine must always saturate at the store: no cascade can exceed it. This forbids infinite loops: caster spells that feed M back into M on a cycle with efficiency < 1 per link diminish (product of efficiencies < 1), so a perpetual loop is impossible (second law).
 6. What else must exist if the coupling exists?
     - Detectors: any structure coupling to M absorbs and senses it. Nerves, brains, crystals with bigger g sense more.
     - Materials with different g and different Z_M, mu_M: conductors/insulators of M. Anti-magic = low g (the structure doesn't couple) or high mu_M absorber.
     - M-heating: dissipated M ends as heat in the absorbing material (booked).
     - Noise: structures have thermal noise in I(x); at small signal, coupling is noisy (the error that gives neighbouring valid spells). Shot noise and thermal noise give a floor; Shannon 1948: C = B log2(1 + S/N) bits/s per channel (a medium with bandwidth B and signal-to-noise S/N). The user's channel table can be filled with B and S/N per medium.
     - Selection: structures that couple strongly get fed and survive (beasts that evolve to harvest M). Evolution acts on the coupling g and on harvesting efficiency.
 7. Consistent symmetries in the sim: ledger rule: the sum over (matter heat, matter kinetic, chemical, M, light) is constant per tick. Add M-channel entries. Check at each tick, as with heat.
 8. Pattern functional I(x): needs a definable measure in the sim. Options: structure complexity score from the data (number of distinct parts and their correlations), or just a data tag per structure class giving g and a "bandwidth" B. Pick the second (cheap). (Design choice, not physics.)
 9. Cheaper estimating laws that keep the facts: treat M as a scalar field on a coarse grid (diffusion, leak, use), treat pulses as ray or segment events with arrival time = distance / c_M and a transmission factor = product of exp(-mu_i d_i) and (1 - R_i) along the path (one ray march, no wave solve). Interference, resonance and standing waves can be handled by a few explicit rules: sum amplitudes for coherent sources, Lorentzian response around f0 with width f0/Q for resonators. All exact in the cases that matter, and cheap.

8. PORTABLE MECHANISMS (general law; data parameters)
======================================================================
P1. M FIELD DYNAMICS. dM/dt = D laplacian(M) + S(x) - k M - U(x,t). Params: D (m^2/s), S (W/m^3 per place), k (1/s), initial M0(x) per region. M = 0 gives low magic. Energy unit: joule; leak goes to heat. Cost: coarse grid diffusion.
P2. PULSE PROPAGATION. A packet of energy E, frequency f, bandwidth B moves at c_M(f). Arrival time = path / vg. Received energy = E * exp(-sum mu_i d_i) * product(1 - R_i) * geometric factor (1/r^2 spread or beam). Params: c_M, dispersion coefficient, mu_M per material, Z_M per material, wavelength rule lambda = c_M/f. Dispersion smear = (dvg/df)*L*B (u).
P3. IMPEDANCE BOUNDARIES (reflection, shields, absorbers). R = ((Z2 - Z1)/(Z2 + Z1))^2; shields at thickness > 3 skin depths, delta = sqrt(2 rho_M / (w mu_M)). Params: Z_M, rho_M (M-resistivity), mu_M per material and band. Absorbed energy becomes heat in the material.
P4. INTERFERENCE LAW. Coherent pulses add in amplitude: I = I1 + I2 + 2 sqrt(I1 I2) cos(dphi). Counterspell zone ~ lambda/10 around the cancelling source; requires matching f and known phase. Energy conserved (redistribution plus canceller's work).
P5. RESONANCE AND STORAGE. Resonator with f0 and Q: response is Lorentzian (width f0/Q); stored energy builds up to ~Q times per-cycle drive; leaks at f0/Q per cycle. Params: f0, Q, volume. Shapers (lens, mirror, waveguide) are P2 and P3 geometry. Focus: spot >= lambda/(2 NA); depth ~ pi w0^2/lambda.
P6. NEAR/FAR FIELD. For r < lambda/(2 pi), coupling is reactive (high efficiency, no radiation, tell hard to detect); for r > 2 D^2/lambda, radiative, 1/r^2. Touch spells vs ranged spells.
P7. METASTABLE STORE AND TRIGGER. State (E_store J/kg, barrier Ea J or nucleation rate J0 exp(-dG*/kT), leak half-life, front speed). Release = min(E_store x mass, available). Trigger needs energy >= Ea or a nucleation seed. Chain factor k_chain >1 grows exponentially, saturates at the store. Critical size from Frank-Kamenetskii number. Covers fuel, explosives, supercooled water, charged crystals and loaded M-pockets, avalanches.
P8. GAIN AND LASER LAW. Output = gain medium (stored M or charge) x cavity (engraved pattern) x seed (trigger). Threshold: loop gain >= 1. Above it, output grows then saturates at the store; efficiency eta applies, waste = (1 - eta) P_in. Params: gain, loss, eta.
P9. TRANSDUCTION LEDGER. Every conversion: E_in = E_work + E_light + E_heat, with eta <= the thermodynamic bound for that conversion (Carnot for thermal sources 1 - Tc/Th; Curzon-Ahlborn at max power; chemical/mechanical at pack-set eta). Chain efficiencies multiply. Params per transducer class: eta, light fraction. Heat is booked at the transducer (caster body or object).
P10. INFORMATION OVERHEAD. Control cost = overhead_per_bit x bits of pattern complexity per cast, floor kT ln2 per bit erased. Output stays limited by the source (P9). Knowledge gain (mutual information) raises extractable work per cast at most kT*I (Sagawa-Ueda), so better knowledge of the gradient helps; param: overhead_per_bit, per individual finesse.
P11. COUPLING AND RECIPROCITY. Each structure class has g (coupling), B (bandwidth in bits/s via Shannon: B log2(1 + S/N)) and noise floor. Sending ability = sensing ability (same g). Recoil: momentum of the packet (E/c_M) goes to the holder of the pattern (8.2). Anti-magic = region or material with low g or high mu_M.
P12. DISSIPATIVE PATTERN (spirit/patron/god). State (boundary integrity, bits, M content). Update: dM_self/dt = intake - leak - repair_cost; intake from sources (ley S, feeders, store); the pattern dies when M_self < threshold or boundary fails. Params: intake W, leak 1/s, repair rate, boundary strength, bit count. Larger patterns need more intake (Kleiber exponent ~0.75 as a default scaling, u). Lasting channels to worshippers are links (P11) with fixed bandwidth.
P13. TOPOLOGICAL OR SOLITON PERSISTENCE (optional). Some patterns persist with no intake while undisturbed: barrier to unwinding E_b; decay rate ~ exp(-E_b/kT_eff). Param: E_b. Covers sealed spirits, enchantments that last centuries (engraved patterns are the same law: a high-barrier fixed pattern).
P14. SELF-ORGANISED CRITICALITY FOR M. With slow drive (S) and threshold release, M builds up in a place and discharges in avalanches (power-law). Parameters: threshold, relaxation rule. Gives "mana storms" and wild magic surges as lawful events, with no extra code (pack option).
P15. SECOND-LAW GUARD. No cycle may extract net work from a single reservoir: M refill is an external source term S with its own booked origin; M must stay out of equilibrium with matter (M above the matter's M-level). Heat pumps for cooling: COP <= Tc/(Th - Tc). Check at the ledger: any tick with net energy gain not matched by S is a bug.
P16. WAVE ARRIVAL COST SHORTCUT. Use ray marching on the grid (P2) rather than a full wave PDE: exact for energy, speed and attenuation; interference done only between declared coherent sources. Real-time-friendly.
