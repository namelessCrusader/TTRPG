2. FOOD SUPPLY, from sources (world.py FIELD block)
Rule: mean supply = mean demand (the steady state of a stable population; Tsimane household production 5,700 kcal a day equals what that household eats, Gurven et al. 2015): counter refill 5 + 4 producers x (1 - 0.10 laid-up) x m = 5 x 3.6; m = 3.61 rations (1.00 of a person-day) per well producer-day.
Variance unchanged from the hard world, from the library: empty catch 0.48 (Tsimane hunts 45 percent, Ache about half), Gamma(2) size, person-day CV 1.1 (Ache) to 1.6 (Tsimane household). Work at the counter is not counted in the mean supply.
Measured in the 60 lives (219,000 person-nights): catches 12.92 + refill 4.99 = 17.91 rations a night vs demand 18.00 (0.99). Well producers' catch mean 3.61, empty 0.49, person-day CV 1.38: inside the source ranges. With work at the counter, 1.10.
Eaten: 16.42 a night = 91 percent of the need (2,189 kcal a person-day). The rest is spoilage: 3.34 rations a night (19 percent of catches, the perishable-catch law, half-life 90 turns, unsourced u). I tried raising production to cover it: spoilage rose with it (smoke runs, lossfit), so no loss factor was added.
Who eats (share of need): Tom 85, Sir Ada 86, Oren 87, Fenn 96, Marta 102 percent. Days under 50 percent of the need: 21-22 percent for the three producers, 16 Fenn, 5 Marta. Days under 75 percent: 35-37 percent. Shortfall comes from the variance, timing, access and spoilage. A day-level shortfall is common (about half of person-days are under 100 percent). I could not find a source figure for the share of household-days under need in the Library response (it gives catch variance, not household shortfall frequency); I did not tune toward one. Say so: item 2's request to match the shortfall frequency to a source is NOT met; only the mean and the variance are sourced.

3. THE BANDIT (old rate 0.6 a night, unsourced)
Sources fix deaths, not arrivals: violent death 0.03 percent (late-medieval Europe, Eisner 2003) to 1 percent a person-year (precontact Hiwi, Hill et al. 2007). No non-lethal threat rate exists in the library.
Derivation: total violence budget R = 1 percent a person-year (top of the band, which is a choice) split half to raids (the raid law halves to HARD_RAID x 0.5) and half to the lone bandit (the split is an unsourced choice; Eisner: most killings were between acquaintances). Arrival chance b = 0.01 x 0.5 x 5 / (365 x d).
d = deaths per arrival, MEASURED (calib_bandit.py: 24,000 fresh nights with a forced bandit, raid nights excluded): 22 people killed in 23,969 nights = 9.18e-4 (Poisson 95% CI 5.8e-4 to 1.4e-3). So b = 0.0746 a night (was 0.6), about 27 arrivals a year. Fresh nights carry no wound in, so d is a floor.
Check: violent deaths in the sweep: 0 in 600 person-years (95% upper bound 6.1 per 1,000 person-years, 0.61 percent). The library band is 0.3-10 per 1,000: the bound sits inside it, but the sweep expected about 3 bandit deaths (3,267 arrivals x 9.18e-4, Poisson chance of 0 is 5 percent) plus raid deaths and saw none. The deaths per arrival in carried lives (people hurt and tended, the cast fighting back as a group) is lower than the fresh-night calibration. Either way the simulated rate is below the top of the band and not above it. I did not retune.
