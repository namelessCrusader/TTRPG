1. THE DEATH LAW (flag "lethal"; lab/world2/world.py, LETHAL_* constants and comments)
One law, no per-cause script. Every need that has a clock has a RESERVE of days. A need held at its extreme (the cap, 1.0) uses up the reserve at 1 day a day.
A need below the extreme gives a day back per day. A reserve at zero is death. Thirst and hunger are two rows of one table.
 - THIRST: 5 days without water in total, from a usual level to death (reserve 4.44 days after the need first reaches the cap). Source range 3 to 7 days (v),
   "rule of threes" literature: Wikipedia "Rule of threes (survival)", Healthline, Live Science (popular summaries; the 3-day figure is traditional, the one-week
   figure comes from end-of-life observations). Mid-range 5. No single clinical paper was read: say (v) with that caveat.
 - HUNGER: 45 days of total starvation on water (reserve 44.25 days). The owner's range 30 to 60. Check (v): the ten 1981 Irish hunger strikers died at 46 to 73 days on water
   and salt (Wikipedia "Hunger strike", "1981 Irish hunger strike"); they were well-fed young men, so 45 is the low end. Secondary sources only.
 - ILL OR HOT: the reserve is used 1 + ail times as fast (ail is 0 to 1, so a full ailment doubles it). (u) Fever raises water loss and metabolic rate, about 13 percent a degree (k);
   the factor is not fitted to anything. There is no heat in the inn, so only the ill part exists.
 - GIVE-BACK: a day away from the extreme returns a day of reserve. (u) No source.
 - UNTREATED AILMENTS. No case-fatality datum for untreated common illness or injury was found (Library or search). The death is DERIVED from thirst and hunger while laid up.
   Sugiyama (2004) ASSUMES that a month or more of disability is fatal without food from others (11 of 17 would have died); that is his assumption, not a measurement.
   Here a laid-up person who is not fed dies of hunger in about 45 days (about 24 with a full ailment) and who is not watered of thirst in about 3 to 5 days.
   Tending lowers the ailment as before (the ailment multiplies the rate, so tending also slows the clock).
 - The body CARRIES from night to night under the flag (hunger, thirst, reserves); without it each night starts fresh as before.
 - Scale note: in this world thirst reaches its cap 0.56 day after a usual level and hunger 0.75 day, so the reserves above are the source totals minus that time.

2. THE PERMANENT-DEATH VARIANT
It did not exist (docs/learned-mind-target.md 9.10 only recommends it). Added as world flag "perma" (same family, LETHAL_FLAGS = lethal, perma). A person who dies is
marked gone in the carry and is born dead every later night (it does nothing, brings no catch, cannot fall ill). Without perma the dead come back next night afresh (the revival
kept for learning). Worlds: all6hl = all6h + lethal + perma; all6hl_rev = all6h + lethal only. The tournament and lifetime loops no longer re-run the death hook for the long dead.
Partners (P-need, P-hard) now also do the SELF-CARE of food and drink asking, buying and working when the world is lethal (lab/world2/partners.py background): without it a scripted
partner starved (Oren died of hunger at night 276 in the first probe). That is an artefact of the script, fixed behind the flag only.

5. OVERALL DEADLINESS (every person of every run, deaths and person-years until death)
P-need runs: 64 deaths in 121 person-years = 527 per 1,000 person-years. P-kind runs: 71 in 118 = 600. Causes (P-need): thirst 34, violence 18, hunger 12.
Library figures: Hiwi accidental trauma about 2 per 1,000 adults a year, accident plus violence 8 to 9 per 1,000, violent death 0.03 to 1 percent a year (Hill et al. 2007). The world is far above them.
Cause 1, THIRST OF THE LAID UP (33 of the 34 thirst deaths were of laid-up people). The inn has no way to bring water to a bedridden person: the well is in the yard, the bucket is
not a pot, and a portioned meal can be asked for or served but a drink cannot. The sources say the bedridden are fed AND cared for (99.4 percent fed). So the death law works as intended and the
world is missing the affordance by which others bring water. This is a content gap, not a rate.
Cause 2, VIOLENCE: 18 deaths in 121 person-years = 148 per 1,000 (Sir Ada 7, Marta 6). The raids are rare now (0.0025 a night); these are the inn's own fights between the cast (rules), about 100 times the
Hiwi violent-death rate. This was hidden before by the next-night revival. It needs its own source-based calibration.
Hunger: 12 deaths, half of them not laid up (counter stock and lumpy catches; not studied).
Laid up at death: 43 of 64. Share of person-nights laid up 5.6 percent (the hard inn says 10; early death thins it).
