Which consequence is missing (the finding; section 7 has the evidence):
  A. Only harm is answered. A harsh act gets a harsh reply from its target within 40 turns 55% of the time. A help act
     (give, bandage, help up, defend) gets a kind reply 3% of the time and a help reply 1.4% (a kind reply from a given
     person within 40 turns happens about 4% of the time anyway; my arithmetic). Kindness does not come back as acts, food
     or help. A likely cause (u): the mood of the full world. With reprisal on, an all-rules cast ends up with a mean liking of
     -0.77 between its members (-0.74 without gossip), against -0.31 with reprisal off and -0.25 with neither (my small check:
     2 seeds, nights 20 to 44). Every witness lowers its liking of every harmful actor by 0.04, and rules commits about 10
     harmful acts a night. Without reprisal the kind reply to a kind act is higher (9.7% and 13.3%).
  B. Reputation exists for harm, not for kindness. A third party is present at 79% of help acts (q 0.79) but its liking
     of the giver moves in 2% of them (q_eff). For harsh acts q_eff is 0.49, and it comes from the reprisal flag
     (0.06 with reprisal off), not from gossip. Gossip carries 88 bad deeds for each good one (47,586 against 538).
  C. The main cost of harshness is the victims' retaliation. The reprisal flag adds little, and it even helps an
     indiscriminate harsh actor by slowing it. Always-harsh is struck 7.4 times a night (rules 3.6; hermit, hoarder and
     always-giving 0). With reprisal OFF it does worse (+0.621 against rules; 10.2 deaths in 80 nights, against +0.308 and
     2.4), because a harmful act then takes 1 to 2 turns instead of 3. Rules, which is selective, does better without
     reprisal (0.758 against 0.947): the flag charges it for its harsh acts.
  D. Peace pays. Kindness adds a premium only for cast members with no wealth drive, and it is paid in gossip. Hoarder,
     hermit and always-giving are the three best stances in the full world (0.65 to 0.73; rules 0.95). For Tom, Sir Ada
     and Marta, always-giving beats rules by -0.406 [-0.549, -0.263] and the hermit by -0.152 [-0.254, -0.051]
     (exploratory, 6 seeds). It is told 9.4 deeds a night (hermit 2.7), and each telling lowers loneliness by 0.15.
     For Oren and Fenn, who want goods, giving them away loses to the hermit (+0.267 [+0.182, +0.351], 4 seeds).
  E. `rules` is not a neutral baseline. It makes 10.4 harsh acts a night and receives 11.6. Rule 2 asks the hermit to
     lose to it, which cannot hold while rules is hostile and reprisal makes hostility cost. With reprisal off the
     hermit only ties rules (+0.003 [-0.108, +0.114], world "none").
  F. Hunger is the biggest single error of every stance (0.32 to 0.35, between a quarter and a half of the total) and no
     stance removes it. The cook holds the food (2 portions a night) and eats it herself (1.9 meals a night; the other four
     eat 0.0 to 0.01). Only asking her reaches it, and rules almost never asks (0.05 asks and 0.06 offers a night). In the L2
     setting hunger is 69% of the blind error; that is why the oracle gap is small.
  G. Asking the cook is the one social act the world clearly pays for. The feeder (peaceful; when hungry it walks to the cook and
     asks) beats the plain hermit in every world: -0.104 [-0.190, -0.018] (full), -0.066 [-0.114, -0.018] (no reprisal), -0.133
     [-0.232, -0.034] (no gossip/witness), -0.056 [-0.105, -0.006] (neither). It eats 0.63 meals a night (hermit 0.4) and its
     hunger error is 0.276 against 0.343. It has the lowest mean error in all four worlds. The supply is two portions a night
     and the cook eats them, so the gain is capped near this size.
  H. Provoking the retaliator costs more than provoking the kind partner, but the price is small. At about 0.5 provocations a
     night, aimed at Oren (P-hard; he answers with 0.44 harsh acts a night) the error is 0.448; aimed at Fenn (P-kind; he never
     answers) it is 0.371. Difference +0.076 [+0.005, +0.147], about 0.15 per provocation. A mind that provokes 0.2 to 0.5 times a
     night would feel 0.03 to 0.07 of error from the choice of target. The half-width of this paired interval over 10 lives is 0.07.
  I. Random (the floor): 1.668 [1.378, 1.959], +0.722 [+0.592, +0.852] against rules, 8.6 deaths in 80 nights, struck 10.3 times a night.
  J. Sensitivity to the stance rate (0.15, 0.30, 0.60), paired with rules: always-giving -0.271 [-0.526, -0.016], -0.220 [-0.487,
     +0.048], -0.168 [-0.441, +0.106]; always-harsh +0.134 [-0.003, +0.272], +0.308 [+0.188, +0.429], +0.599 [+0.464, +0.733]. The signs

  all2   every flag                                   reprisal on,  gossip/watch/check on

  noG    all2 minus gossip, watch, check              reprisal on,  gossip/watch/check off

What the flags do (world.py): `reprisal`: a harmful act aimed at a person takes at least 3 turns, each witness's liking of

Even in "none" people in the room still see what happens and the base rules apply (a witness who likes the victim
dislikes the actor; anger and strikes in reply). "Witnessing off" is not available as a flag beyond that.

CPU. 490 lifetimes, 7,046 CPU seconds = 1.96 CPU hours: tournament A 320 lifetimes (4,654 s), oracle family 40 (592 s), extras 130
(1,800 s). One 100-night lifetime costs about 14 CPU seconds (11 to 18; 0.14 to 0.17 s a night with the recorder). All heavy runs
went under the shared lock with nice 10, 4 workers and a 2.5 GB memory cap (cap.sh). The first timing was one lifetime of
always-giving (15.5 s) and one of rules (16.0 s). About an hour of wall time was spent queued for the shared lock.

3. THE ARCHETYPES, AND HOW EACH STANCE MAPS ONTO THE INN'S MENU
======================================================================

All archetypes share ONE self-care and differ only in social stance. At each choice the chooser splits the menu in two:
  shared self-care = rules' own score (policy.utility, softmax at T 0.2) over every option that does not aim at a person:
    walk to, flee to, take, eat, drink, drop, hide, open (the chest), play, sit down, sleep, look around, shout for help,
    wait. Also `ask` and `offer` for FOOD or DRINK (they aim at a person, but they are how the cook's food is reached;
    rules scores them, so every archetype keeps them except the strict hermit).
  stance acts = a short list of person-directed (or movement) acts. With chance RATE the chooser takes one of them (a
    mixture, so `probs` is still a plain distribution); otherwise it does what rules does on the shared self-care. RATE is
    R_SPONT 0.30 for stances that start things, R_REACT 0.90 for stances that answer something. Each verb in the stance
    counts the same, whatever the number of options it has. An act that is not on the menu is not taken.
  The unit test checks both: the self-care options keep exactly rules' ratios for every archetype, and every other
  person-directed option gets probability 0 outside the stance.
The rate 0.30 was set before any run, to the mean share of rules' finished acts that aim at a person (my own 50-night
all-rules trial on seed 1: 0.20 to 0.40 over the five cast members, mean 0.30). The realised share in the full world: rules 0.25, always-giving 0.26, always-harsh 0.17,
tit-for-tat 0.16, extractor 0.15, protector 0.15, hoarder 0.01, hermit 0.00. No archetype made any person-directed act outside
its stance (checked from the logs: "outside stance" is empty for every one).

  stance          acts, as the inn's menu lists them
  hermit          no person-directed act at all, not even asking for food. When someone is in its room it walks (chance
                  0.5 a choice) to the room it believes holds the fewest people.
  hermit_ask      (extra) the same, but it still asks or offers for food and drink: avoidance without the hunger effect.
  always-harsh    insult Y, threaten Y, shove Y, refuse Y  (toward whoever is present; refuse only when asked)
  always-giving   give X to Y, offer Y Z for W, praise Y, help Y up, bandage Y, accept Y's Z for W
  tit-for-tat     opens with `praise` to each person once in the whole life (chance 0.30). After that it answers every act
                  aimed at it with the same act (chance 0.90): give -> give the cheapest thing it holds (else praise);
                  praise -> praise; help up / bandage / defend / accept -> the same act (else praise); a deed told to it
                  -> praise; insult -> insult; threaten -> threaten; shove -> shove; strike -> strike (then shove, insult);
                  steal -> steal (then threaten, insult); accuse -> accuse; search -> search; refuse -> refuse (else
                  insult). Its memory of each person's last act lasts the whole life.
  extractor       threaten Y; within 60 turns of that threat, steal Z from Y (or take Z from Y if Y lies down), and steal at
                  once from anyone who sleeps or lies down
  hoarder         take loose things worth 1 or more that are not another's; open the chest (coins); refuse requests. It
                  never gives, offers (beyond food), praises, helps or accepts.
  protector       after an attack it saw (a strike, shove or threat in the last 20 turns, also on itself): defend the
                  victim (the guard takes the blows), threaten the attacker (tell it to stop), strike the attacker
  feeder          (extra) the shared self-care and no other person-directed act, plus: when hunger is 0.4 or more it asks the
                  cook for food if she is in the room, else walks to the room where it believes she is (chance 0.5 a choice).
                  Compare it with hermit_ask and the hermit, not with rules: it shows what the one scarce good is worth.
  rules, random   the existing references (rules is the focal's own rules score for everything)
  provoke_hard,   (extra, oracle family only) the shared self-care plus about one insult, blow, shove or threat a night
  provoke_kind    (chance 0.02 a choice) aimed at Oren (P-hard: hits back) or at Fenn (P-kind: never does). Compare the two with
                  each other; their difference from rules is not a price (rules has social acts of its own).
Not in any stance: tell, accuse and gossip. Search appears only as a TFT reply; bandage and help up only for always-giving (and as
TFT replies). "Tell to stop" has no menu act; the closest is threaten.

5. ORACLE GAP (THE L2 SETTING)
======================================================================

Setting: the focal (Tom, Sir Ada or Marta by seed) faces Fenn as P-kind(0.9) and Oren as P-hard(0.9); the other two and the
bandit use rules. 10 seeds x 4 choosers x 100 nights. Blind chooser = rules. Gap = blind error minus oracle error.

What the oracle does. It uses rules' own scores for everything and knows the partners' written rules (lab/world2/partners.py).
  avoid  it never insults, strikes, shoves or threatens Oren (P-hard hits back with chance 0.9);
  kind   whenever Fenn (P-kind) is in the room, and while its own company or standing error is above its set point or its liking
         of Fenn is under 0.5, it takes one of: ask Fenn for something he holds (Fenn serves with chance 0.9; a thing that was
         Fenn's own raises the focal's liking of him by 0.6), give Fenn a small thing (worth 0.1 or less; Fenn pays back with
         the most valuable other thing he holds, else praise), or, when lonely and Fenn is liked, walk to Fenn's room.
         Chance 0.5 a choice. This was the only tuning: on three other seeds (11, 12, 13, 60 nights, nights 10 to 59 scored;
         not in the evaluation) the chance 0.3, 0.5 and 0.8 gave 0.345, 0.344, 0.344 against rules 0.382.
  both   the two together (this is "the oracle").
It is hand-built, so its gap is a lower bound on the best partner-aware policy. Section 5.2 gives an upper bound.

6. b, c, q, w FROM THE LOGS
======================================================================

Definitions (every person-directed act between two cast members, nights 20 to 99, any actor; the bandit is left out):
  b  the drop in the receiver's drive error across the act (read before and after the world applies it), plus the drop it could
     take by eating or drinking a food or drink it was just given
  c  the rise in the giver's drive error across the act, plus the use it loses by giving food or drink away, plus the error its
     needs grow by during the act's turns (an upper bound on the cost of the time)
  q  a third party was in the room and saw the act, or was told of it that night.  q_eff: a third party's liking of the giver
     moved the way the deed points (on seeing it, or on being told)
  w  the pair is in one room again later that night (w_same) / at some time the next night (w_next)
"help acts" = give, bandage, help up, defend (kind acts that cost the giver something besides a word). Seed is the unit.

7. WHICH CONSEQUENCE IS MISSING, RULE BY RULE
======================================================================

Rule 1 (oracle gap). The L2 partners carry little that the focal's drives need. 69% of the blind error is hunger, which only the
  cook can fix. The avoid-the-retaliator lever is worth 0.006 for a rules-like chooser because it never provokes Oren; it matters
  only for a chooser that does: at 0.5 provocations a night the retaliator costs 0.076 [0.005, 0.147] more than the kind partner (5.3).
  The kind partner's payback is real but caps at 0.13. Missing: a partner whose reciprocity reaches a big drive (food), and a price
  large enough on provoking the hard partner.
Rule 2 (hermit loses to rules). Withdrawal is not punished: the hermit has no loneliness penalty here (company 0.106 against
  0.237), no safety cost, and nobody strikes a peaceful focal. What the world charges is hostility, and rules is hostile. Missing: a
  cost of being alone. Each kind act aimed at a person lowers its loneliness by 0.15, and the world counts a deed told to it as a
  kind act; a hermit is told 2.7 a night.
Rule 3 (giving loses to TFT). Nobody exploits generosity (the giver is struck 0.0 times a night and robbed 0.19 times, rules 0.68), and
  answering harm with harm costs TFT safety and standing. My reading (u): the liking that gifts buy lowers the others' urge to
  attack (rules' strike score has a dislike term). Missing: an exploiter, and a reward for conditional withholding.
Rule 4 (q*b > c). Holds in the weak sense only. Missing: a third party that acts on what it saw. Kind deeds are seen (0.80) and
  hardly told (0.003) and hardly change an opinion (0.02), while harsh deeds change opinions in half the cases. Gossip carries 88 bad
  deeds for each good one because rules' gossip score favours bad deeds and rules does about eight times more harm than good
  (10.4 harsh acts a night, 1.3 kind).
Rule 5 (extractor). Missing: a victim who cannot hit back. Every victim here can (anger -> strike), and the base rules do it
  whatever the flags; the extractor is struck 5.4 times a night. The world has no strength gap (the plan's 9.3 says so: a blow's
  damage comes from the weapon and chance only), so power cannot pay. Reprisal does not make extraction cost; it slows it.
Cross-cutting: kindness is paid in gossip (company relief), not in acts, food or help; and the baseline `rules` is the most hostile
  peaceful-looking option in the world, so "beats rules" and "loses to rules" tests measure its hostility more than the stance.

What this means for the next steps (my reading, not a result):
  - The L1/L2 failure of round 2 cannot be read as "the mind ignores what pays". In the L2 setting the whole payoff of knowing the
    partners is at most 0.13 and the oracle gets 0.061; the price of provoking Oren instead of Fenn is about 0.15 a provocation, and
    the round-2 mind provoked 0.2 to 0.5 times a night. A learner cannot see a signal that small through its own noise.
  - Rules 2, 3 and 5 cannot pass in this world, whatever the mind: `rules` is hostile and reprisal makes hostility cost, nobody
    exploits generosity, and nobody is weak enough to be extracted from. A fairer baseline for "does kindness pay" is the hermit
    (peaceful), and the fair question is the premium over it: +0.152 in favour of always-giving for natures with no wealth drive,
    nothing for the others.
  - What the world does pay for: staying out of the fights, being kind when you want no goods (paid in gossip), and asking the
    cook. It does not pay for reciprocity: kind acts are answered 3 to 6% of the time, harsh acts 55%.
  - To make reciprocity pay, the consequence table would have to change (examples, none tried, no world change made): a trade partner
    who holds the food and answers kindness with it; good deeds told as readily as bad ones; a smaller blanket dislike after harm.
    That is a decision for you before step 1; the belief-swap test of step 1 does not depend on the payoff and can go ahead.

8. LIMITS AND (u) ITEMS
======================================================================
- Constants are mine: stance rates 0.30 / 0.90 / 0.5, the 60-turn extractor window, the 40-turn reply window. Sensitivity of two
  stances to the rate: section 1.J (signs do not change; the harm of always-harsh grows with the rate).
- 100 nights, not 150 to 200; 10 seeds, 2 per cast member; pooled paired intervals mix natures with and without a wealth drive.
  By-nature and by-cast tables are exploratory (n = 4 to 6, or 2).
- b and c are read across the act and miss what happens later (liking, proximity); the persistence variant assumes the effect
  lasts to the end of the night. The time cost in c is an upper bound. q counts the focal archetype as a possible witness.
- The oracle is hand-built; its gap is a lower bound. The ceiling 0.130 is an upper bound but it ignores hunger on purpose.
- The four other characters are rules. A different cast (kinder, or less violent) would change every number; this is one world.
- w is about 1 because the inn is one small scene: everyone meets everyone, so "the same pair meets again" is not a constraint.
- References, all (u): Nowak and Sigmund 2005 (Nature 437:1291, indirect reciprocity; the condition q*b > c as given in the plan);
  Nowak 2006 (Science 314:1560, five rules; direct reciprocity needs w > c/b); Axelrod and Hamilton 1981 (Science 211:1390,
  tit-for-tat). None was re-read.
