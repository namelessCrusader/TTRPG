UNEVEN LUCK for the inn (2026-11-20)   user decision 1: luck first, the other three needs only if luck is not enough

VERDICT IN ONE LINE
Luck is built, tested, and run in two settings. It changes the inn (empty nights really cost a person) but it does NOT flip
any pass rule. Same 1 pass out of 5 as all3 (always-harsh loses to rules). Nothing was tuned: both luck settings were
chosen before the runs, the first runs are the reported runs.

1. WHAT WAS ADDED (one mechanism, one flag "luck", lab/world2/world.py; flags "all4" = all3 + luck)
Half of the night's expected food stops coming from the house pot and comes as each person's own CATCH instead (foraged,
hunted or earned). Everybody who is not the house (Tom, Sir Ada, Oren, Fenn; the cook Marta runs the house) gets one, with the
same expected size (0.5 x 20 rations / 4 = 2.5 rations), but the size varies night to night and independently between people.
  dial v (World(luck=v), env MINDL3_LUCK) = chance of an empty night. Otherwise the catch is Gamma(shape 2) scaled so the
  mean does not move (mean when not empty = 2.5/(1-v)). CV of one catch: v=0.3 -> 1.07, v=0.5 -> 1.41, v=0 -> no variance
  (an even split, control).
  A catch is a food the person holds, by the portion (the same rules as the pot): eat it, SERVE a portion of it to someone,
  others can buy from it or work for it. It cannot be handed over whole. It does not survive the night (each night is a new world),
  so a lucky night's surplus is worth nothing to its holder unless it is shared. That is the Kaplan-Hill logic.
  The house pot shrinks to 6 + 4 rations. The draws come from a generator of their own (seed only): they take nothing from the
  world's own draws, are the same for every chooser playing the same night (paired runs stay paired), and are independent
  between people (tested).
Real-world grounding of the size (all (u), from memory, not re-checked): Ache hunters bring home no meat on about 40 percent of
days and daily returns have a CV near or above 1 (Kaplan and Hill 1985, food sharing among Ache foragers); Hadza and !Kung
returns are similarly lumpy (Hawkes, O'Connell, Blurton Jones 2001; Cashdan 1985). v=0.3 (CV 1.07) and v=0.5 (CV 1.41) bracket it.
No-trait check: nothing reads a name or trait; every person gets the same draw rule; catches go through the existing portion,
serve, eat, buy and work paths.

4. DID LUCK DO ANYTHING (a check of the mechanism, not a pass rule)
Yes, empty nights cost, and giving buys some of that back. Focal error on nights the focal had a catch vs an empty catch
(nights 20-99, 10 seeds, files diag_v03.txt, diag_v05.txt):
  v=0.3   hermit 0.353 vs 0.581 (+0.23)   rules 0.424 vs 0.582 (+0.16)   tit-for-tat 0.470 vs 0.526 (+0.06)   always-giving 0.277 vs 0.316 (+0.04)
  v=0.5   hermit 0.299 vs 0.567 (+0.27)   rules 0.402 vs 0.572 (+0.17)   tit-for-tat 0.438 vs 0.557 (+0.12)   always-giving 0.323 vs 0.392 (+0.07)
So a giver is hurt about 4 times less by a bad night than a hermit: a small, real insurance effect, in the right direction.
It is too small to reach the group totals. Reasons, in order:
  (a) Everyone's error went DOWN, not up (hermit -0.12): the catch is extra food at hand that a hermit can eat alone, and the
      house still sells a meal for a thing worth 1-3 or an hour of chores ("buy" and "work", 17,000 acts each). A market substitute
      for kindness is always open, so hunger is still met without a partner. The share partners can touch stays tiny (oracle
      ceiling 0.016 of the error, as all3 0.020).
  (b) Oracle gap: the oracle partner policy still cannot recover more than 0.016 of the error. Rule 1 needs a need that ONLY
      another person can meet, and food is not it while the house stays open.
  (c) Always-giving still beats tit-for-tat (-0.12 to -0.16, not significant): giving costs the giver almost nothing (c 0.02) and
      is answered (reply_kind 0.58-0.63), so conditional kindness has no edge. Luck did not raise the price of giving, only the
      value of getting.
  (d) Extractor: still loses everywhere including reprisal-off; the cast's harsh replies, not the reprisal term, defeat it (as in
      the inn report, (u) not isolated).

5. WHAT IS MISSING (not tuned for)
The luck variance is real, but the world has a cheaper way out than asking a friend: the house pot and the "work for a ration"
counter. Luck gives a need but not a need that ONLY a person can meet. The user's other three needs (decision 1 said: only if luck
is not enough) are now the open question: luck is not enough alone. The closest to what the data points at:
  - a need the market cannot meet (a danger one cannot survive alone, a good that needs two), or
  - a house that closes (no counter for the unlucky: the pot is the lucky ones' only food source), or
  - a cost to giving that is not negligible (c is 0.02 now) so that conditional kindness can beat unconditional.
Not tried here (outside the one-mechanism brief). Each is one more mechanism, so each is your call.
