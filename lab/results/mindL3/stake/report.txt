WHAT MAKES RECIPROCITY PAY: THE LIBRARY'S MEASUREMENTS, THE FIXED PASS RULES AND THREE NEW WORLD LAWS (2026-11-20)

VERDICT IN ONE PARAGRAPH
No pass rule flipped. Old rules: 1 of 5 in all5 and 1 of 5 in all6 (always-harsh loses to rules; nothing else). The three new laws are built,
tested and run, but the cast that plays in them (four rules characters) does not use them: its error went UP (rules focal 0.385 -> 0.628),
while always-giving and tit-for-tat did not move. The fixed rules say: (1) the oracle rule cannot pass, its upper bound is 0.068 (all5) and
0.095 (all6) of drive error, below 0.1; (2) the hermit ties the cast, as the library expected (hermit minus cast -0.014 in all5, -0.121 in all6,
both CIs hold 0); (3) with an extractor in the cast, always-giving does not lose to tit-for-tat, it BEATS it (-0.29 and -0.24, CIs below 0).
The measurements show where the textbook conditions stand: the care acts pass them, the pooled number does not (section 2 below). Nothing was
tuned: every size was fixed before any run, and the first runs are the reported runs.

1. THE THREE NEW WORLD LAWS (each its own flag; all6 = all5 + the three; the same rule for every person)
------------------------------------------------------------------
lumpy   Lumpy, perishable catches (S2.4). With chance 0.30 a person's day is a hit and brings the whole expected catch at once, one package of about
        8 rations (2 need units; a person needs 0.9 a night), at a turn 1-150; otherwise nothing. The expected catch is unchanged (2.4-2.5 rations).
        Fresh food spoils: half-life 90 turns (u: warm weather, a night = 300 turns = a day). The house's cooked staples keep. One new fact in the
        giving rule: "spare", the share of a pot the holder could not eat before it spoils; it takes the cost of a giveaway off by that share.
        Own random generator (seed only), so every chooser sees the same nights.
stock   A finite counter stock (S2.5). The house's stew and bread carry from night to night, are capped at twice the nominal pot, and are refilled
        only by people's work: the cook makes 5 rations a night and every "work" at the counter puts 2 back after the worker takes 1.
        DESIGN COST (stated before the runs, shown by the ablation): the old counter was refilled to 10 rations by magic each night. Now it
        starts a night at 5.1 rations on average (10.0 without the flag), so the flag also cuts the counter's supply by half. Both sizes (5, 2, cap 2x) are mine (u).
choosy  Partner choice extended to help acts (S2.3, S6). The distrust term that already sat in serve (weight 2.0, from the partner rule: liking, deeds heard,
        lies, robberies) now also weighs on tend, defend, bandage and help up. "Fend off" is left out: it picks no person. Reads no trait.
(d)     Tending and defending are not buyable. Test: in 900 random turns of all6 only food is ever bought or worked for, no tend/defend/bandage option
        carries a price. Measured: providers of care or defence through the counter = 0.00 in every cell (section 3).
Tests: lab.world2.test_world 64 passed (56 before, flags-off included, + 8 new); lab.mindL.test_tournament 17 passed (+2: the recorder changes nothing in
all6; the ablation worlds drop exactly one flag; family E has an extractor). The all5 re-run with the new logs equals the stored all5 in 80 of 80 runs.
Added to the lab tournament: worlds all6, all6R0, no_lumpy, no_stock, no_choosy; family E (always-giving or tit-for-tat with an extractor in one of
the other four seats, all5 and all6, seeds 1-10, 20 each); read-only logs (every person's error each night, catches, who ate what, counter stock,
providers, who tended whom, spoilage).

The hermit tie condition (Hauert, Monte, Hofbauer, Sigmund 2002, doi:10.1126/science.1070582, from the library's reading, u): with an opt-out paying sigma the
time-average payoff of every strategy equals sigma, and cooperation lives only if 0 < sigma < r - 1. Here the hermit's payoff IS sigma. A tie means the counter alone
pays what company pays: the gain from company, r minus sigma, is zero. "The hermit loses" can pass only where that gain is above zero. It is not: the cast gives a
hermit the same care (it gets 0.25 care acts a night against 0.11 for a rules person in all6) and the same food.
Rule 3 fixed: the extractor is in one of the other four seats. Tit-for-tat pays more: 2.3 deaths in 80 nights against 1.5 for always-giving (all5), 2.5 against 1.2 (all6).
My reading (u, not tested): tit-for-tat answers a theft with a theft and a blow with a blow and is drawn into fights; always-giving takes the loss and keeps out of them.

5. DID EACH LAW DO WHAT THE LIBRARY SAID  (mechanism checks, not pass rules)
------------------------------------------------------------------
lumpy   Yes at the act, no in the cast. Serve got cheap for the holder and valuable for the hungry: c/b 0.21 -> 0.11, b x2.6. But the rules cast hardly serves: a rules
        focal serves 0.10 a night in all6 (0.36 without lumpy, 0.53 without stock) against 2.2 for always-giving and 2.3 for tit-for-tat. Serve is scored only when the
        receiver is over 0.6 hungry, and the rules score has no term for the giver's spare food beyond cheaper cost. So the packages rot in the rules cast (31%), and its
        error rises. The extractor, hermit and hoarder also lose (+0.07, +0.07, +0.03): they cannot reach the packages either.
stock   Partly. The counter ends the night empty in 97% of nights (92% in no_stock, so it was never an endless faucet inside a night). A focal with no catch on a night the
        counter ended empty had error 0.810 against 0.548 on other nights in all6 (0.646 against 0.516 in no_stock). People hardly refill it: 0.12 work acts a night for a rules
        focal in all6 (0.50 without the flag, where the counter was full). So the cast does not "work to refill the counter"; the supply cut is what the flag mostly does.
choosy  It did the opposite of what was meant. The distrust term reads harm done, not help withheld. The rules cast quarrels, so its members distrust each other and care
        is held back inside it: a rules focal gives 0.14 care acts a night and gets 0.11 in all6 (0.50 and 0.36 without choosy). The hermit, nobody's enemy, gets 0.25 and
        gives 0. Packages eaten by someone else fell from 3,839 to 2,400 rations. Nothing in anyone's history records "this person never helps", so a free rider is still
        tended (S6: unconditional care is what learners exploit).
(d)     Confirmed: care and defence cannot be bought (0.00 counter providers, menu test).

6. WHICH CONSEQUENCE IS STILL MISSING, IN S7 TERMS
------------------------------------------------------------------
- b/c and the thresholds are not the problem for care: b/c about 8.5, q_eff 0.64-0.72, w 0.99 all pass. The pooled fail is the zero-sum "give" (c/b 1.1, 63% of acts).
- Stake: s is 0.12-0.25 already and the world changes did not move it. A stake exists; nothing links it to the choice of whom to help.
- Providers: care has 3.9 free providers for everyone, hermit included, and none at the counter. The partner rule's weight (2.0 x distrust) bites only on someone
  distrusted for harm. The hermit has no harm history, so it stays served. This is why the hermit ties (sigma = r) and why always-giving is not punished.
  The missing consequence is a difference between persons in what their history with a partner contains, for a person who gives nothing. Whether that can come
  from the existing records (liking raised by help received, deeds heard) without a reciprocity rule is the open question; I did not add one.
- Pooling: R = 0 and predicted u/s 0.50, but the cast realises a tenth to a quarter of it; the food rules (serve only at hunger over 0.6) is what leaves packages to rot.
- Oracle: with 4.5-4.8 kind acts a night and (b-c) of 0.015-0.020 a night, the world cannot show a 0.1 gap to partner knowledge. To pass, kind acts must be more
  numerous or each worth more (care b 0.12 per tend act, but tend is 4% of acts).
